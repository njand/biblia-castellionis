### B. Castellio's text departs from the Hebrew (kind C)

1. **4:5** *habitāns sub palmā Dēboræ, inter Hormam et Bēthēl*: Hebrew בֵּין הָרָמָה וּבֵין בֵּית־אֵל, "between Ramah and Bethel"
   (the NRSVue "between Ramah and Bethel", the BSB the same, neither with a note; the KJV "between Ramah and Beth-el", the
   Vulgate *inter Rama et Bethel*). Hormah (חָרְמָה) is another town, in the Negeb far to the south, which Castellio himself
   names at 1:17 (*Hormamque nōminārunt*, for חָרְמָה); Deborah's palm stands in the hill country of Ephraim, by Ramah of
   Benjamin. Elsewhere Castellio gives Ramah as *Rāma*: Judg 19:13 *Gabaæ aut Rāmæ* (settled), 1 Samuel in 1750 *apud
   Ramam* (OCR lines 40629, 41017). So a name changed into another place's name, not a spelling of his: not the "names
   spelled his way" the process leaves out. Not a printer's slip: 1738 *inter Hormam et Bethel* (`text/orig/JDG.usfm`
   line 136; OCR line 34090), 1750 *inter Hormam et Bethel* (OCR line 33950), 1573 *inter Hormam & Bethel* (MDZ canvas
   173). The form of the existing kind-C notes (*Hebraicē* Hebrew, ‘Latin’), the name with the macron of the settled 19:13.
   **Near the bar** only in that it is a place name; the sense of the verse (where Deborah judged) is what changes.
   **Apply:** `Judg.4.5` la: `inter Hormam et Bēthēl,` → `inter Hormam\f + \fr 4:5 \ft Hebraicē הָרָמָה, ‘Rāma’.\f* et Bēthēl,`

### B. Castellio's reading departs from the Hebrew (kind B)

Both stand in 1738, 1750 and 1573: Castellio's own text, so noted, not corrected.

1. **15:19** *fissō māxillæ dente molārī*: Hebrew וַיִּבְקַע אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הַמַּכְתֵּשׁ אֲשֶׁר־בַּלֶּחִי, "God split the hollow place
   that is in Lehi" (the NRSVue "So God split open the hollow place that is at Lehi", the BSB "God opened up the hollow
   place in Lehi", neither with a note; the NET "the basin at Lehi", its note only on *basin*, "a circular-shaped
   depression in the land's surface": not disputed). Castellio follows the Vulgate (*Aperuit itaque Dominus molarem
   dentem in maxilla asini*): the water springs from a tooth of the jawbone Samson has just thrown away, not from a
   hollow at the place Lehi. Brenton's LXX τὸν λάκκον τὸν ἐν τῇ σιαγόνι ("the pit in the jaw") and the KJV's "an hollow
   place that was in the jaw" keep the hollow, so the tooth is the Vulgate's (and the rabbis') reading. His own verse
   ends *quī fōns in Māxillā est ad hunc diem*, the place, as 15:9 and 15:14 *Māxilla* for Lehi; the note uses his own
   name for it. מַכְתֵּשׁ is a common enough word ("mortar", Prov 27:22; a hollow, Zeph 1:11), so this is Deut 26:5's case
   (a Vulgate reading of a plain Hebrew), not a rare word. 1738 *fisso maxillæ dente molari* (`text/orig/JDG.usfm` line
   551; OCR `sources/archive/1738/text.txt` lines 35980–35981), 1750 the same (lines 35959–35960), 1573 *fiſſo maxillæ
   dente molari* (canvas 181, MDZ search).

### C. Castellio's *G.* … *H.* mark at 16:13–14 (weighed: recommended left out)

3. **16:13–14** *et clāvō parietī affīxeris … nec ejus vīrēs cognitæ sunt*: 1738 prints *G.* before *et clauo parieti
   adfixeris* (16:13) and *H.* after *nec eius vires cognitæ sunt* (end of 16:14) (candidates file: 1738 OCR *G. et /
   clauoparietiadfixeris* and *fit G. ex pariete, nee eius vires co- / gnirx funt. H.*; 1750 the same, *G.* … *H.*). The
   tool placed the *H.* at 16:15; it closes 16:14. The Hebrew 16:13 ends with "the web" (עִם־הַמַּסָּכֶת) and 16:14 opens
   "and she fastened it with the pin". **The NRSVue and the BSB both add, from the Greek, nearly all of what Castellio
   marked**: the NRSVue "and make it tight with the pin … into the web" (note "Compare Gk: Heb lacks *and make it tight
   . . . into the web*"), the BSB 16:13 "and tighten it with a pin, I will become as weak as any other man" and 16:14 "So
   while he slept … into the web" (notes "See LXX and Vulgate; Hebrew does not include …"). What Castellio has beyond
   them is (a) the wall: *clāvō parietī affīxeris*, *clāvō fīxit in pariete*, *revulsit ex pariete*, as Brenton's LXX
   ἐγκρούσῃς τῷ πασσάλῳ εἰς τὸν τοῖχον … ἐκ τοῦ τοίχου (the modern Bibles fasten the pin in the loom); and (b) the closing
   *nec ejus vīrēs cognitæ sunt*, which Brenton's LXX doesn't have (his source for it not identified; it repeats 16:9).
   **Why left out:** Castellio here goes the modern Bibles' way (Gen 4:8, 21:9), and Gen 44:6, a *G.* … *H.* reading the
   NRSVue also adds, was left out (`gen-notes.md` §C); the two details beyond them leave the story as the reader of the
   NRSVue reads it (the hair woven, the pin, Samson waking and tearing it out). Unlike Exod 2:22, 6:20 and Num 10:6,
   nothing marked is absent from the modern Bibles' story. If noted all the same (Exod 28:34's precedent, a mark of his
   own), the wording would be:
   **Apply (only if accepted against the recommendation):** `Judg.16.14` la: `nec ejus vīrēs cognitæ sunt.` → `nec ejus vīrēs cognitæ sunt\f + \fr 16:14 \ft Ā ‘et clāvō parietī affīxeris’ (16:13) ad ‘cognitæ sunt’ Castelliō ex Græcō vertit.\f*.`
