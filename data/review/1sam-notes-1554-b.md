Notes on the readings:
- **17:4**: the Hebrew read on the zoom `z825-s17heb.jpg`: *בנים*, then *à*, then *בן* with a final nun. Castellio derives
  אִישׁ־הַבֵּנַיִם (the "champion", literally "man of the between") from בֵּן, "a man of sons, i.e. of men, i.e. outstanding",
  hence *immānis*. A construal of one word; the sense (the Philistines' great fighter) is the Hebrew's. No note.
- **17:12**: he emends בָּא בַאֲנָשִׁים ("advanced among men") to בַשָּׁנִים ("in years"): the NRSVue does the same ("Gk Syr: Heb
  *among men*") and the BSB ("LXX and Syriac"). No departure from the modern Bibles.
- **20:19**: explains *ter* (Hebrew וְשִׁלַּשְׁתָּ, "do it a third time" = on the third day); the NRSVue calls the verse
  uncertain. Commentary.
- **28:17**: "I preferred to read לך, which is in the margin, as Jerome and the Greek translated, rather than לו." The WLC
  has לוֹ with no marginal reading; the NRSVue "The LORD has done to you" (no footnote), with him. His Bible's "margin"
  is not identified here (unverified: perhaps a printed Hebrew Bible's marginal variant). No note.
- **30:2**: he follows the Greek "the women and all who were in it", as the NRSVue ("Gk: Heb lacks *and all*") and the
  BSB ("LXX; Hebrew does not include *and all*"). No note.

**None proposed.** Every lemma matches the preview's words (spelling aside).

## 1738's asterisks are these annotations

1738's seven footnote-less asterisks in chapters 16–31 (phase 1, `1sam1-31-transcription-readers.md`) stand at the end
of these seven lemmas, one each: 17:4 after *immanis*, 17:12 after *prouecta.*, 20:19 after *ter*, 25:20 after
*descendenti,*, 25:27 after *comitantur,*, 28:17 after *tibi,*, 30:2 after *mulieres*. Canvas 825's other seven
annotations (1:3, 6:8, 6:18, 8:12, 12:17, 13:1, 14:36) fall where 1738's other seven asterisks stand (reader A's). So
1738 marks with an asterisk every place Castellio annotated in 1554, without printing the annotation. (Ruth's 3:16
asterisk, "not proved" in `ruth-notes-1554.md`, is the same mark.) 1750's OCR shows none of them.

- **All twelve of 1738's footnotes in 16–31 are Castellio's 1554 margins**, changed only in spelling and stops (*paullo*,
  *adfirmare*, *cælum*, *quum*, *scil.*; 25:31 *Nabale* → *Nabalem*; 1554 letters chapter 25's notes a–d on one page,
  1738 a; a–c across two leaves).
- **28:6 is complete**: 1554 ends *gestabat in monili.*; 1738's final comma is its own slip, not a note broken off.
- **16:6**: 1554's *hic, ô Ioua* ("this one, O LORD", glossing *apud Iouam eius unctus*) is not among 1738's footnotes.
- None marks a departure from the Hebrew; decided in `1sam-notes-b.md` §E.

## The 1554 text against the settled text (beside the notes)

- **20:30 (d)**: 1554 *O' improbæ, inquit, contumacisq; matris progenies* ("O son of a wicked, contumacious mother"),
  read on the zoom `n165-20_30.jpg`; 1573 the same (*O c improbæ*, canvas 201, OCR); 1738 *o improbe* (and 1750, Wikisource).
  Castellio's margin (1738's own footnote l) explains *improbæ*. A later printer's change, against the author's sense and
  his note: proposed for the verse review in `1sam-notes-b.md` item 4.
- **28:6**: 1554 *per Claritatem* with a capital (1738, 1750 *claritatem*), the Urim, as Exod 28:30 *CLĀRITĀS*. For the
  verse readers; not a note.
- **17:43, 17:49, 22:9**: the *G.* … *H.* marks stand in 1554 as in 1738 (canvas 163, column 301: *me baculo? G. &
  lapidibus H. aggrediare? G. Non, inquit Dauid, sed cane deterior. H.*; *ut ille G. transadacta galea, & H. affixo
  fronti saxo*; canvas 166, column 307: *Achitobi filiũ. G. L. pontificẽ, H. qui pro illo Iouam cõsuluit*), and in
  1573 (canvas 199) at 17:43 and 17:49.
- **17:12–31, 17:55–18:5** and the other verses Brenton's LXX lacks: all in 1554 (canvases 162–163).
