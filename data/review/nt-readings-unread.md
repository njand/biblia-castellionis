# Thirteen more slips in the settled New Testament, found by reading the unread words of 1573

Proposed 2026-11-15. The printed-text build had filled about 1,650 New Testament words with the settled word, where
1573's OCR was unreadable, read another word, or had none (`data/review/nt-orig-accents.md`). All 1,655 were read on
the page images of **1573** (MDZ bsb10141277, the New Testament base, decision 24): 166 contact sheets, five
readers, the doubtful ones zoomed at full resolution by me. 1,612 were the word the draft has (spelling, accents
and marks now recorded in `data/decisions/<book>.orig.tsv`); 37 were another word, missing, or joined. Six of those
are the slips already proposed in `data/review/nt-readings-1573-1556.md` (Luke 20:21, John 9:27, 14:16, 16:30,
Acts 11:16, 14:23), found again independently. The rest are below.

Each was checked in **1556** (e-rara 10281656, Castellio's last lifetime edition): in its OCR
(`sources/erara-1556/<page>.txt`) and, where that was too rough, on its page image. Where 1556 was not found, I say
so. **Your decision**, item by item (`accept 1, 2, 4 …`).

## Proposed: the settled text follows 1573

1. **Matt 24:2** *nūllus lapis super lapide* → 1573 *lapis super lapi-|dem*, 1556 *lapis super lapidem*
   (page 10282328): the accusative, as both printings.
   **Apply:** `Matt.24.2` la: `lapis super lapide relinquātur` → `lapis super lapidem relinquātur`
2. **Mark 1:28** *in omnia Galilæa fīnitima* → 1573 *in omnia Ga-|lilææ finitima*, 1556 *in omnia Galilææ* (OCR
   *galilex-*, page 10282333): "into all the places bordering on Galilee", the genitive.
   **Apply:** `Mark.1.28` la: `in omnia Galilæa fīnitima` → `in omnia Galilææ fīnitima`
3. **Luke 3:17** *arcamque suam perpūrgābit* → 1573 *areamque suam perpurgabit*, 1556 *areamq; suam perpurgabit*
   (OCR, page 10282347): *ārea*, the threshing floor (Greek ἅλωνα), not *arca* "chest".
   **Apply:** `Luke.3.17` la: `arcamque suam` → `āreamque suam`
4. **Luke 9:42** *convellit et lacerāvit* → 1573 *cõuulsit & lacerauit*, 1556 *conuulsit & lacerauit* (OCR,
   page 10282353): the perfect, as *lacerāvit* beside it (*convellō, convulsī*).
   **Apply:** `Luke.9.42` la: `accēdente, convellit et` → `accēdente, convulsit et`
5. **Luke 10:11** *vōbīs abstergēmus* → 1573 *vobis abstergimus. Cæterùm*: the present, "we wipe off". 1556 not
   found in its OCR (not looked at on the page).
   **Apply:** `Luke.10.11` la: `vōbīs abstergēmus` → `vōbīs abstergimus`
6. **Luke 23:12** *Factīsque sunt inter sēsē amīcī* → 1573 *factiq; sunt inter sese amici* (canvas 811, zoomed),
   1556 *factiq; sunt* (OCR *sactity*, page 10282363): *factīque sunt … amīcī Pīlātus et Hērōdēs*, "and Pilate and
   Herod became friends"; *Factīsque* is not a construction.
   **Apply:** `Luke.23.12` la: `Factīsque sunt` → `Factīque sunt`
7. **John 4:29** *fortasse eum esse Chrīstum* → 1573 *fortassis eum esse Christum*: as Castellio has it in the
   settled text's three other places (Luke 20:13, Rom 5:7, Phlm 15). 1556 not found in its OCR. Every other witness
   has *fortassis* too (OCR 1681, 1715, 1750, 1758; Wikisource; WordPress 2019); *fortasse* came from `TXT/3 -
   Edited` alone, the earlier hand-edited stage. Classical evidence (2026-11-15, `pipeline latin`): both are
   classical. Lewis & Short gives *fortassis* its own entry; Gaffiot and Wiktionary list both. The Latin Library
   corpus has *fortasse* 1,518 times (Cicero 435) and *fortassis* 431 (Erasmus 199), the classical ones in Plautus
   (*Asinaria*, *Bacchides*), Horace (*Sermones* I and II), Ovid (*Ars*), the two Plinys, the elder Seneca and Seneca
   (*Quaestiones naturales*), and the *Declamationes maiores*, with none from Cicero. So *fortasse* is far more
   common, but *fortassis* is classical, which is enough for decision 18.
   **Apply:** `John.4.29` la: `fortasse eum` → `fortassis eum`
8. **Acts 2:21** *Dominī nōmen invocāverit, ēvādet* → 1573 *nomen inuocauerit, serua-|bitur*, 1556 *ac quicunque Domini
   nomen inuocauerit, seruabitur* (OCR, page 10282381): another verb, "will be saved" (σωθήσεται).
   **Apply:** `Acts.2.21` la: `ēvādet` → `servābitur`
9. **Acts 8:23** *Nam ingeniō contumācī tē amārō felle præditum et injūstitiā cōnstrictum esse videō* → 1573 *Nam
   ᵃtibi acerbam imminere bilem, & te scelere constrictum esse video*, with the margin note *a ingenio cõtumaci*;
   1556 the same sentence (OCR, page 10282386: *nam tibi acere bamimmiinerebilem,& te scelere consiricium eile
   uideo*), and Castellio's own annotation on it (page 10282528) explains *nam tibi acerbam imminere bilem* from the
   Greek, *uideo tibi acerbam bilem, hoc est, graues a deo portias imminere* (OCR as read; *portias* is
   presumably *pœnas*, not looked at on the image). 1573 has the same annotation (canvas 839, OCR: *Nam tibi acerbam
   imminere.) In Græco est … Video tibi acerbam bilem hoc est, graues à Deo pænas immine-|re*), and its text line
   was read on the page (canvas 839): *Nam ᵃtibi acerbam imminere bilem, & te ſcelere conſtrictum eſſe video.* The settled sentence is Wikisource's (the early line, `data/calibration/scans.md`); Castellio's final
   text is another sentence, "for I see that bitter gall awaits you, and that you are bound by wickedness".
   Macrons: *ăcerbus*, *immineō, ēre*, *bīlis* (Gaffiot, through `pipeline latin macron`).
   **Apply:** `Acts.8.23` la: `Nam ingeniō contumācī tē amārō felle præditum et injūstitiā cōnstrictum esse videō.` → `Nam tibi acerbam imminēre bīlem, et tē scelere cōnstrictum esse videō.`
10. **Acts 15:36** *revīseret, quōmodo sē habērent* → 1573 *reuiserẽt, quomodo se haberẽt*, 1556 *reuiserent,
    quomodo se haberet* (page 10282391, read on the image): Paul and Barnabas both, the plural.
    **Apply:** `Acts.15.36` la: `explānāssent, revīseret` → `explānāssent, revīserent`
11. **Acts 18:25** *sōlum Jōhannis baptismum* → 1573 *solùm Iohannis baptisma*, 1556 *solum Iohannis baptisma*
    (page 10282393): Castellio's word, *baptisma* (10 times in the settled text, *baptismate* 9; *baptismum* only
    here and at Rom 6:4).
    **Apply:** `Acts.18.25` la: `Jōhannis baptismum intellegēns` → `Jōhannis baptisma intellegēns`
12. **Rom 6:4** *per baptismum ad mortem sepultī* → 1573 *per baptisma ᶜ ad mortem sepulti*, 1556 *per baptisma ad
    morte sepulti* (page 10282403): as item 11.
    **Apply:** `Rom.6.4` la: `per baptismum ad mortem` → `per baptisma ad mortem`
13. **Acts 26:16–17** *dēmōnstrābō. Vindicābō tē ā populō* → 1573 *demonstrabo, vindicãdo te à populo*, 1556
    *demonstrabo, uindicando te a populo* (page 10282399): the gerund, "by rescuing you from the people", runs on
    from verse 16.
    **Apply:** `Acts.26.16` la: `tibi dēmōnstrābō.` → `tibi dēmōnstrābō,`
    **Apply:** `Acts.26.17` la: `Vindicābō tē ā populō` → `vindicandō tē ā populō`

**Decision:** accept all (Nathan, 2026-11-15; item 7 after checking the classical evidence)

## Not proposed: 1573 differs, the settled word stays (the printed text has 1573's)

Recorded so you can overrule any of them.

- **Acts 7:51** *Dūrī cervīce* (1573 and 1556 *ceruici*): the ablative in *-ī* is not classical; *cervīce* by
  decision 18.
- **Mark 3:34** *circumsedēbant* (1573 and 1556 *circumsidebant*): *circumsedeō* is the classical verb (decision 18).
- **Luke 20:46** *accubitiōnēs* (1573 and 1556 *accubationes*): Gaffiot knows only *accubitiō*.
- **Acts 15:3** *Phœnīcem* (1573 and 1556 *Phœnicen*, the Greek accusative): the settled text has *Phœnīcem* in its
  two other places, where 1556 also prints *Phœnicen*; both are classical. Changing all three is an option.
- **3 John 1** *Gāiō* (1573 *Caio*; 1556 not looked at): the settled text has *Gāius* in its five other places.
- **2 Cor 12:7** *tundat* (1573 *tondat*, 1556 *tundat*): 1573's misprint ("shear" for "beat").
- **Heb 7:5** *genus dūcentēs* (1573 *genus sacerdotium ineunt*, without *ducentes*; 1556 *genus ducentes*, page
  10282450, read on the image): 1573 dropped the word.

now whatever is decided here (decision 58), except **Acts 8:23**: its words are the settled sentence's until item 9

19:6, *crucifigam* 19:15, *crucifigant* Heb 6:6, *econtrariò* 1 Pet 3:9), prints *explorate. An non* at 2 Cor 13:5,
and has the misprints *imumbrabit* (Luke 1:35) and *celebrẽ* (Luke 5:29); all recorded as printed.
