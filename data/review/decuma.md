# The tithe: *decuma* and *decumō*, by Golden Age usage

Proposed 2026-11-13 (Claude), on Nathan's question ("What do the Golden Age statistics have to say about *decimātis*
vs *decumātis* (and related forms)? Once you decide this, …"): decided by decision 18, as with *dextra* and
*miseritus* (decision 59).

The settled text spells the tithe both ways: the noun *decumās* 6 times (Gen 14:20, 28:22; Heb 7:2, 4, 8, 9) and
*decimās* once (Luke 18:12); the verb always with *i* (*decimātis* Matt 23:23, Luke 11:42; *deciment* Heb 7:5;
*decimāvit* Heb 7:6; *decimātus* Heb 7:9). The ordinal *decimus*, *decima* ("tenth": *decima hōra*) is a different
word and is not touched.

- **The noun.** Golden Age authors (Cicero, Caesar, Sallust, Nepos, Varro, Livy, Lucretius, Catullus, Vergil, Horace,
  Ovid; every example of *decumas*, *decumae*, *decumis*, *decumarum* and the same with *i* in `latin attest`):
  *decum-* 165 (Cicero 158, the Verrines' *decumae*; Livy 6; Caesar 1) against *decim-* 8 (Caesar 2, Livy 5, Ovid 1),
  and those 8 may be the ordinal. Gaffiot heads *dĕcŭma*.
- **The verb.** Neither spelling in Golden Age authors. Later Latin has *decimo* 12 times and *decumo* once. Decision
  18 takes the spelling most consistent with the other choices where the counts are too thin: the verb is made from
  the noun (to take the *decuma*), so *decum-*. Gaffiot heads *dĕcŭmō*; `latin macron` *decumātis*, *decument*,
  *decumāvit*, *decumātus*. 1573 prints *decumatis* at Matt 23:23 (canvas 769, page image).
- **Result: *decum-* throughout**, noun and verb, and a stem rule (`decim` is not touched: *decimus* stays).

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-13: "Once you decide this")
