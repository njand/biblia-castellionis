# 2 Kings 19–25: proofreader p4 (2026-11-23)

I read every verse of 19–25 (191 verses) in `text/draft/2KI.preview.usfm` (built 06:42 from the merged decision files and
the draft fixes §1–4) against 1738 (`text/orig/2KI.usfm`), the BSB with its layout, the KJV's ¶s, and the readers'
reports r7–r9 and `names.md`.

**Words.** A script compared the preview's words with 1738's, folding case, macrons, æ/ae, i/j and u/v, and checked
each difference against the approved rules of `data/rules/orthography.tsv`. Every difference is an approved rule or a
listed draft fix:
- **Draft fixes:** 23:13 *Chamōssō* (§4.3) and 25:17 *cubitūs* (§1.5); 19:5 *Ezechīānī* is 1738's word (§1.4 withdrawn);
  19:13 *Sepharvaimī* and 23:11 *Pharvārīm* (§3) match 1738 once u/v is folded.
- **Rule changes:** *quum, quumque → cum, cumque*; *eis, eisque → iis, iisque*; *audiverit, audivisti, audiverat →
  audierit, audīstī, audierat*; *adscendit, adscendebant, adscensurum, adgressus, adloquens, adlocuti, adlocutus →
  asc-, agg-, all-*; *intelligant → intellegant*; *diversorium → dēversōrium*; *repetiit → repetīvit*; *caussa →
  causa*; *coenaculi → cēnāculī*; *simulac → simul ac*; *adseclis → adseculīs*; *Ioannes → Jōhannēs*; *quotidiano →
  cotīdiānō*.

I found no omission or misreading. The punctuation differs from 1738 only in readers' rows: 19:20 (semicolon → colon),
19:33 (colon → comma), 20:10 (parentheses → commas), 20:15, 20:16, 20:19 (*X: words, inquit:* → *X, “Words,” inquit,
“…”*).

**Macrons: 6 forms rows** (`p4-forms.tsv`):
- 19:24 ***effōdī***: the perfect (*Ego effōdī et ēpōtāvī*), as the settled *effōdērunt*; the preview had *effodī*, the
  present passive infinitive.
- 19:27 ***exitūs***, ***ingressūs***: accusative plurals (*tuōs exitus, tuōs ingressus nōvī*), as the preview's own
  *tractūs* 19:23, *gradūs* 20:10.
- 25:22 ***Nabūchodonosor***: the preview had *nabūchodonosor*, though r9's merged layout row keeps 1738's capital.
  `propose`'s case rule lowered it after 25:21b's comma, because the lexicon knows a lower-case *nabuchodonosor* (M). A
  decided form with a capital stops the rule (`keeps_capital`, as Josh 19:37 *Cedes*).
- 25:23 ***Cārēæ***: `names.md` lists *Cārēæ* (קָרֵחַ qamets, tsere) among 25:23's names but wrote no row; the
  preview kept *Careæ*.
- 25:23 ***Netōphātīta***: the names reader's *Nethōphātīta* row assumes draft fix 4, which was left for Nathan, so it
  matches no word and the preview has *Netophatita* unmarked. My row gives the same quantities to the spelling as it
  stands. **If Nathan takes fix 4, drop my row.**

**Layout: 3 rows** (`p4-layout.tsv`):
- **22:15** *illa iīs sīc fāta est: “Sīc dīcit Jōva …*: a colon where 1738 has a comma before Huldah's words. The
  settled text has a colon after *fātus/fāta est* before every speech (*fātus est: “* 12×, *sīc fāta est: ‘Mittam*),
  and no comma there. r7's 19:20
  and the book's other speeches without *inquit* (19:2, 19:6, 19:9, 20:14) do the same.
- **22:18** *sīc dīcit Jōva Deus Isrāēlītārum: ‘Quod …*: a colon where 1738 has a full stop (r8's alternative). The
  settled *Sīc dīcit Jōva …* has a colon before every quotation it opens (6×), and so does this book at 19:20, 21:12
  and 22:16.
- **23:15** a paragraph at ***Āram quoque apud Bēthēl***, where Josiah's reform moves from Judah to Bethel and Samaria.
  This is the KJV's ¶ and the BSB's b m, and r9's first alternative. 23:10–20a ran to 264 words, over 1 Kings' measure
  (as p1's 1:4, p3's 18:36); now 23:10–14 has 111 and 23:15–20a 153. r9's 23:10 paragraph stays.

Everything else stands:
- **Quotation marks:** balanced in 19–25 (21 “, 21 ”; 14 ‘, 14 ’), nested correctly throughout. The five ‘…’ flags
  are right:
  - **19:20**: the Lord's word *‘Quod mē … exaudīvī.’* closes inside Isaiah's still-open message, which runs to 19:34.
  - **19:34**: reopened after *inquit Jōva,* (19:33), lower case as the sentence goes on; *‘… Dāvīdem.’”* closes both
    levels.
  - **20:17–18**: *“‘Scītō … rē,’ inquit Jōva. ‘Quīn … spadōnēs.’”*. Isaiah speaks the Lord's words, and *inquit Jōva*
    is Isaiah's, not the narrator's (the Hebrew's "says the LORD"), so the outer “ is right, as the settled 2 Sam 12:7.
  - **22:20**: *‘Itaque … illātūrus.’”* closes the oracle and Huldah's speech; *Hæc rēs postquam relāta rēgī est:*
    stays outside.
  - **Huldah, 22:15–20**: r8's nesting fits Castellio's Latin better than the BSB's. *dīcite hominī, ā quō ad mē
    missī estis* has *mē* = Huldah, so these are her words, not the Lord's. The Lord's first person begins at 22:16
    *Ego*, after the second *sīc dīcit Jōva:*.
  - 19:23–24: Sennacherib's boast in “…” inside ‘…’, as the BSB.
- **Poetry 19:21–34**: I agree with r7, and it should not be cut back to 21–28. The BSB sets 19:29b–31 and 19:32b–34
  as verse with the same `b m` openings, and Castellio's Latin goes member by member there. The prose frames
  (*Tū vērō, Ezechīā, hoc signum habētō.* 19:29a; *Quārē sīc prōnūntiat Jōva dē rēge Assyriæ:* 19:32a) are `\m` as the
  BSB has them. The lines follow Castellio's own stops.
- **Chapter breaks:** 21:1 *annōs tum nātus*, 22:1 *annō ætātis suæ*, 23:1 *cūrāvit*, 25:1 *annō ejus rēgnī* each open a
  `\p` in lower case after the comma or colon that ends the chapter before. This follows the model of 1 Kgs 3:1 and
  p2's 12:1, p3's 16:1.
- **Paragraphs:** all open at Castellio's sentence starts apart from those four breaks. The largest are 19:20–34 (303,
  the oracle: poetry, broken by its two `\m` frames), 22:20b–23:9 (216), 20:12–21 (207) and 22:14–20 (181).
- **Capitals:** none lower case besides 25:22 above; *Assyriī* 19:11, 19:17, *Bēthēlēnsem* 23:17, *Samarītānīs* 23:19,
  *Hemāthēnsī*, *Hierosolymītānō* 23:33, *Ægyptiī* 24:7, *Chaldæī*, *Hierichūntiō* 25:5 as the readers set them. Lower
  case after a question mark is kept as printed (19:22 *in quem*, 19:25 *urbēs*, 20:14 *aut unde*).

**Left as they stand:**
- 23:12 *eārumque pulverem illinc prōjectum cucurrit in torrentem*: as printed. It looks like the Vulgate's *cucurrit
  inde*; the Hebrew וַיָּרָץ is "and he ran/broke them down". It is not a slip.
- 25:24 *quibus quōrumque hominibus*: as printed (*quōrum + que*).
- The readers' judgments: r7's 19:20 colon; r8's 20:20 out, 21:16 in and 21:18; r9's 23:4 out, 23:10, 23:20b, 23:29,
  23:30b, 24:14, 25:21b. They are sound.

**Spelling gaps.** These are new; p1–p3 listed theirs, and the readers r7–r9 noticed most of these. The preview keeps
them as 1738 prints them:
- ***ad-* unassimilated**, with related forms approved: 19:7 *adflābō* (`adflabo → afflabo`; the approved *adflaret,
  adflatus, adflatu …*, the settled *afflāret, afflāvit*); 20:5 *adscendēs* (`adscendes → ascendes`; beside 20:8
  *ascēnsūrum* by rule, as p1's *adscend-*); 23:16 *adferenda* (`adferenda → afferenda`; the approved *adferendum,
  adferendae*); 21:14 *adspernātusque* (`adspernatusque → aspernatusque`: the approved `adspernatus → aspernatus` lacks
  the *-que* form).
- ***-mt-* for *-mpt-***: 20:13 *prōmtuārium* (`promtuarium → promptuarium`; the settled *prōmptuāriō* ×3).
- ***sēpt-* for *sæpt-***: 25:4 *mūrō utrimque sēptam* (`septam → saeptam`; the approved `septique → saeptique`,
  `circumsept → circumsaept`, and the settled *sæpta, sæptō, sæptīque*).
- Not proposed: 19:3 *in arctum* (r7): the settled text keeps *arctā* once, so it is Nathan's call whether *arct- →
  art-*. 22:6 *lātumiīs* (r8; Gaffiot *lautumiæ*): Castellio's spelling, for Nathan.
