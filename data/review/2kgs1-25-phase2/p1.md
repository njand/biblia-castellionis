# 2 Kings 1–6: proofreader p1 (2026-11-23)

I read every verse of 1–6 (174 verses) in `text/draft/2KI.preview.usfm` (built 06:42 from the merged decision files and
the draft fixes §1–4) against the BSB with its layout, the KJV, the Hebrew, the Vulgate and 1738 (`text/orig/2KI.usfm`).

**Words.** A script compared the preview's words with 1738's. It folded case, macrons, æ/ae, i/j and u/v, and checked
each difference against the approved rules of `data/rules/orthography.tsv`. Every difference is an approved rule or a
listed draft fix (`2kgs1-25-draft-fixes.md`):
- **Draft fixes:** 1:1 *Moabitæ*; 2:19 *quemadmodum*; 5:26, Castellio's *G. L. … H.* dropped and *ut emās* kept.
  3:9 *circuīvissent* and 3:16 *alveō* match 1738 once u/v and i/j are folded.
- **Rule changes:** *quum → cum*; *eis → iis*; *adscend-, adgress-, adlocut-, adloquitur, adpareo, adspectu, adferrent,
  adponam, adpone, adponit → ass-, agg-, all-, app-, asp-, aff-*; *simulac → simul ac*; *eumdem, quemdam → eundem,
  quendam*; *sumto → sumpto*; *epistola → epistula*; *caussam → causam*; *petierunt, petiit → petīvērunt, petīvit*.

I found no omission or misreading. Every punctuation difference from 1738 (12 verses) has a reader's layout row, apart
from 5:26's dropped mark.

**Spelling gaps: nine words need a rule.** The preview keeps 1738's *ad-* in them, though the approved rules
assimilate related forms. Decision 37 gives the rule. *adfutūrum* 3:7, *adfuit* 4:36 and *adfuerim* 5:26 are compounds
of *sum*, so they stay. *adstante* 4:12 and *adstantem* 4:15 follow the approved *astan- → adstan-*.
- 1:6 ***adscendissēs*** and 1:16 ***adscendistī***. 1:4 and 2:11 have *ascendisset* and *ascenditque* by rule, and the
  settled text has *ascendisset, ascendissent* …. Proposed: `adscendisses → ascendisses`, `adscendisti → ascendisti`.
  The settled text also has one *adscendisse*, which may itself be a gap.
- 2:20 and 4:41 ***adferrī***. The approved rules have *adferre → afferre*, and 6:2 has *afferrent*. Proposed:
  `adferri → afferri`.
- 2:20 ***adlātō***. The settled forms are all *allāt-*. Proposed: `adlato → allato`.
- 3:20 ***adfluēbat***. Proposed: `adfluebat → affluebat`.
- 4:41 ***adfundī***. Proposed: `adfundi → affundi`.
- 4:42 ***adpōneret***, beside *appōnam, Appōne, appōnit* 4:43–44 by rule. Proposed: `adponeret → apponeret`.
- 4:9 ***adsiduō***. The approved rules give *assiduus, -ī, -am, -um, -īs, -ē*; *adsiduo* alone is missing. Proposed:
  `adsiduo → assiduo`.

r1 and r2 found the same gaps.

**Macrons: 1 forms row** (`p1-forms.tsv`):
- 4:10 ***dīversētur***. *Dīversor* is the frequentative of *dīvertō*, and the preview marks *dīvertēbat* 4:8 and
  *dīvertisset* 4:11 beside it. The tool gives W *dīversor*. The preview had the form unmarked. The settled Matt 21:17
  *diversātus* is also unmarked. For Nathan: change it to *dīversātus* by hand so the two agree.

I checked every other word in context:
- **Ablatives** (most of them the readers' rows): *ā Jōvā*; *eāque convolūtā*; *sublātā … lænā*; *pluviā vīsā*;
  *īnstrātā asinā*; *famē*; *Hāc lēctā epistulā*; *leprā*; *porrēctā manū*; *ēmersāque secūrī*; *validā manū*;
  *in mediā Samarīā*.
- **Perfects against presents:** *pervēnit* 1:13; *vēnit* 5:9, 5:15; *venit* 4:32, 5:25 (historic present, r2);
  *subvenit* 6:27 (present, r3); *prehendit* 4:27; *dēciderat* 2:13; *dirēmērunt* 2:11; *comēdimus* 6:29.
- ***hīc/hic*:** *hīc* 3:10, 3:11, 4:29 (adverbs); *hic* 5:7, 6:17 (pronouns).
- **Subjunctives and futures:** *vīderis* 2:10 and *offenderis* 4:29 (future perfects); *percontārēre* 1:16;
  *complectēris* 4:16 (future); *pūrgēris* 5:13.
- **Other forms:** *istīus* 2:9; *currūs* 2:12; *exercitūs* 4:13, 5:1; *domūs* 5:9; *salīnum* 2:20; *sāl/salem*
  2:20–21; *pēnsitābat* 3:4; *dēlēctum* 3:6; *intellēctūrum* 5:8; *comēssēmus, comēsūræ* 6:28; *parsit* 5:20 (W,
  as settled); *Undenam* 5:25, *undenam* 6:27 (unmarked, as settled 8×).

`macron-check` on 1–6 flags only settled conventions: *Jōva, Ēlīam, Jorām-, Bēthēl, usque, quæsō, Mōabīt-, sēque*.
Its 93 unknown words are names, æ/œ words and forms checked above.

**Layout: 1 row** (`p1-layout.tsv`):
- 1:4: a new paragraph inside the verse at ***Hīs ad nūntiōs ab Ēlīā perlātīs,***.
  - **Why:** 1:1–12 ran to 264 words, over 1 Kings' measure.
  - **Where it comes from:** the KJV's ¶ at 1:5, moved to where Castellio's sentence begins. This is also where 1738's
    own verse 5 opens. r2 did the same at 5:19, and 1 Kgs 15:8 has the same kind of move.
  - **Result:** 1:1–4a has 85 words and 1:4b–12 has 179.
  - **The alternative:** r1's 1:11 would leave a 54-word paragraph.

Everything else stands:
- **Quotation marks:** balanced in 1–6 (92 “, 92 ”), with no nested ‘…’. Speeches that run over verses close
  correctly (1:13–14, 3:14–15, 3:16–19, 5:11–12, 5:17–18, 5:26–27, 6:28–29).
- **Reported speech:** unmarked as the readers set it.
- ***inquit*:** set off with commas throughout.
- **Lower case after a question mark:** kept as printed at 3:13, 4:43, 5:13 and 6:27.
- **Paragraphs:** all open at Castellio's sentence starts. The largest are 4:18–31 (248 words), 3:9–19 (221),
  6:24–33 (221) and 6:13–23 (210).
- **Poetry:** none, as the BSB sets none.

**Left as they stand:**
- 2:3 *Scisne*: *scīs* is long, but the settled text has *Scisne* twice, unmarked, and W lists *scisne* unmarked. It
  is kept for one form. If Nathan wants *Scīsne*, it should change everywhere.
- 2:23 *Ascende calve, ascende calve*: no comma before the vocative, as printed.
- 6:22 *Quōs tū … cēperis, occīdās licet*: read as a future perfect, so the short *-eris*. The perfect subjunctive
  of attraction, *cēperīs*, is possible beside *occīdās licet*, and Nathan can choose it.
- 4:16 *nōlī mihi tuæ impōnere*: *tuæ* agrees with *mihi* ("me, your servant", Hebrew בְּשִׁפְחָתֶךָ), as printed.
- The readers' judgments: r2's 4:32 paragraph and the one inside 5:19, and r3's 6:13 paragraph. They are sound and
  under the measure.

I found no printer's slips beyond the draft fixes.
