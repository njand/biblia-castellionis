## A. Modern Bibles follow the versions (kind A)

None proposed. The places where both the NRSVue and the BSB leave the Hebrew:

- **20:13** *Quibus dēlectātus Ezechīās*: the Hebrew וַיִּשְׁמַע עֲלֵיהֶם, "and he listened to them"; the NRSVue "Hezekiah welcomed
  them" (note "Gk Vg Syr: Heb *When Hezekiah heard about them*"), the BSB "And Hezekiah received the envoys". Castellio
  is with them, and says why in his 1554 annotation: וישמע *malè legitur pro* וישמח (Isa 39:2's word, "he rejoiced"),
  as the story and the old translation show (`notes-1554-b.md` §1). Brenton's LXX *ἐχάρη*, the Clementine Vulgate
  *Lætatus est*. No note.
- **13:6** *recessērunt, quīn ea imitārentur*: the plural, with the NRSVue ("but walked in them", note "Gk Syr Tg Vg:
  Heb *he walked*") and the BSB (its note the same). With them.
- **19:31** *Jōvæ armipotentis studiō* (the Qere's "LORD of hosts", with both) and **19:37** *ipsīus fīliī* (the Qere's
  "his sons", with both). With them.
- **20:4** *quī nōndum mediam urbem ēvāserat*: the Kethiv הָעִיר, "the city"; the NRSVue ("the middle court", no note) and
  the BSB ("the middle courtyard", note "LXX and an alternate MT reading; the other alternate reads the middle of the
  city") take the Qere. Kind A by the letter, but where Isaiah was when the word came is no matter of plot: dropped by
  the rule of 2026-11-19 (a place). The KJV "middle court", the Vulgate *mediam partem atrii*.
- **20:12** *Berōdacus Baladam*: the Hebrew's *Berodach* (both modern Bibles "Merodach-baladan", the BSB's note "Some
  Hebrew manuscripts, LXX, and Syriac (see also Isaiah 39:1)"). A name, Castellio with the Hebrew: dropped. *Baladam*
  is 1554's spelling (`notes-1554-b.md` §4).

## C. Places near the bar (recommended left out)

**Decision:**

1. **23:29** *Hic Jōsīās rēx, cum Pharaōnī Nechōnī, Ægyptī rēgī, adversus Assyriæ rēgem in expedītiōnem ad flūmen
   Euphrātem proficīscentī, īvisset obviam*: Neco marching **against** the king of Assyria. The Hebrew עָלָה פַרְעֹה נְכֹה …
   עַל־מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר, "went up to (or against) the king of Assyria"; the NRSVue "went up to the king of Assyria to the River
   Euphrates" (no note), the BSB "marched up to help the king of Assyria" (no note); the KJV "against", the Clementine
   Vulgate *contra regem Assyriorum*, Brenton's LXX *ἐπὶ βασιλέα Ἀσσυρίων*. Castellio's sense is the old versions'; the
   modern Bibles read Neco as Assyria's ally. 1554 (canvas 219, column 414: *Aegypti re-|gi, aduersus Assyriæ regem in
   expeditionem*), 1738 and the preview alike.
   **Near the bar, left out**: a construal of the preposition עַל, which can bear either sense, and nothing in the book
   turns on it: Josiah goes out to meet Neco and is killed either way, and Assyria is not heard of again. As 1 Kgs 8:12
   (a construal, left out 2026-11-22), not as reader A's 3:27 (where the story's turn differs).
   **Recommendation (Claude): leave out.**
   **Apply (only if accepted against the recommendation):** `2Kgs.23.29` la: `adversus Assyriæ rēgem in expedītiōnem` → `adversus Assyriæ rēgem\f + \fr 23:29 \ft Hebraicē עַל־מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר, ‘ad rēgem Assyriæ’.\f* in expedītiōnem`

2. **25:19** *et Sōphrem exercitūs ducem, quī dēlēctūs habēre solitus erat*: Castellio takes הַסֹּפֵר, "the scribe", for a
   man's name, *Sōphrēs*, the army's commander. The Hebrew וְאֵת הַסֹּפֵר שַׂר הַצָּבָא הַמַּצְבִּא אֶת־עַם הָאָרֶץ; the NRSVue "the secretary who
   was the commander of the army who mustered the people of the land", the BSB "the scribe of the captain of the army,
   who had enlisted the people of the land", the KJV "the principal scribe of the host". The Clementine Vulgate has the
   name too: *et Sopher principem exercitus, qui probabat tyrones*. 1554 (canvas 220, column 416: *& So-|phrem exercitus
   ducem, qui delectus habere solitus erat*), 1573 (canvas 265, search API: *& Sophrem exercitus ducem*), 1738 alike.
   **Left out**: one man is taken and killed either way (25:20–21), and the NRSVue itself makes the secretary the
   commander; only his title becomes a name. A name, dropped by the rule of 2026-11-19. **Recommendation (Claude):
   leave out** (no Apply line).

3. **16:6** *Razīn, Syriæ rēx, Ēlatham (exāctīs inde Jūdæīs) recuperāvit Syrīs, eōque colōnia Syrōrum dēducta est*: the
   Kethiv וארמים, "and the Arameans came to Elath"; the Qere "Edomites", which the BSB ("sent the Edomites into Elath")
   and the NRSVue take, the NRSVue also making Rezin recover Elath "for Edom" (note "Cn: Heb *Aram*"). Castellio keeps
   the Hebrew's written text, against the Clementine Vulgate (*et Idumæi venerunt in Ailam*) and Brenton's LXX
   (*καὶ Ἰδουμαῖοι ἦλθον*), which read the Qere. 1554 (canvas 214, column 403: *recuperauit Syris, eoq; colonia Syrorũ deducta est*, read at 1200 px), 1738 alike. A people
   settled in a town, nothing turning on it; and Castellio is with the Hebrew's letters. **Recommendation (Claude):
   leave out** (no Apply line).

## D. 1738's footnotes (recommended: none printed)

**Decision:**

1738 prints 5 lettered footnotes in chapters 13–25 (`%` rows of `data/witnesses/read-1738/2Kgs1-25.tsv`; its letters
restart at 18:17). As in Joshua through 1 Kings and reader A's chapters, the edition prints no glosses (brief.md: "No
marginal notes or commentary"). **All five are Castellio's 1554 margins** (`notes-1554-b.md` §3). Each, with what it
glosses:

- **18:17** *a) scilicet extra vrbem.* (on *apud aquæ ductum*: the Assyrians halt outside the city).
- **19:3** *b) tantus est noster dolor, quantus parturientis feminæ, quæ ad eiiciendum fetum iam ad vuluæ fauces
  progressum, vires non habet.* (on *jam fētū in arctum prōgressō*: the image of the woman in labour explained).
- **19:24** *c) magna.* (on *aggeribus mūnīta flūmina*: "great" rivers).
- **19:25** *d) istarum vastitatum auctorem esse, de quibus tu te iactas.* (on *mē hoc ōlim ita comparāsse*: God the
  author of the ruin the Assyrian boasts of).
- **21:13** *e) sic Hierosolymam puniam, vt etc.* (on the measuring line and plummet of Samaria and Ahab's house).

None marks a departure from the Hebrew. **Recommendation (Claude):** print none.
