The yield is **1 note proposed in 12 chapters** (0.08 a chapter), and it is near the bar: 3:27, where Castellio turns
the Hebrew's "great wrath against Israel" after the Moabite king's sacrifice of his son into the Israelites' own horror
at the deed. No kind A passes. Castellio's one mark (5:26 *ut emās*) and his one addition defended in an annotation

## A. Modern Bibles follow the versions (kind A)

None proposed. The places where both the NRSVue and the BSB leave the Hebrew are 9:27 ("and they shot him", §C item 4),
10:16 ("he had him ride": Castellio's *Illō in currum ēvectō* names no subject, so it sits with both), and, by the
letter, 8:16 (§C item 5). Everywhere else one of the two keeps the Hebrew (§F).

## B. Castellio's text departs from the Hebrew (kind C)

**Decision:**

1. **3:27** *cēpit fīlium suum nātū maximum … eumque super mœnibus immolāvit. Hāc tantā atrōcitāte permōtī Isrāēlītæ,
   ab eō discēdunt, et in suōs fīnēs revertuntur*: "the Israelites, **shaken by so great an atrocity**, withdrew". The
   Hebrew וַיְהִי קֶצֶף־גָּדוֹל עַל־יִשְׂרָאֵל וַיִּסְעוּ מֵעָלָיו, "and there came great wrath upon Israel, and they withdrew from him";
   the NRSVue "And great wrath came upon Israel, so they withdrew from him" and the BSB "And there was great fury against
   the Israelites, so they withdrew", neither with a note, nor with any note calling the Hebrew uncertain; the KJV
   "great indignation against Israel". Castellio's sense is the old versions' as he could read them: the Clementine
   Vulgate *facta est indignatio magna in Israël* (which can be Israel's own indignation), Brenton's LXX *ἐγένετο
   μετάμελος μέγας ἐπὶ Ἰσραήλ*, "great remorse"; his 1554 has no annotation here.
   **Why it passes the bar (near it)**: it is **theological matter and the turn of the story**. In the Hebrew the
   sacrifice is followed by a wrath that falls on Israel and drives its armies home, whoever's wrath it is (the
   commentators' old question); in Castellio nothing falls on Israel: the Israelites leave because they are appalled. A
   reader comparing a modern Bible meets "wrath against Israel" and finds no trace of it in the Latin. Against it: it is
   a construal of a short Hebrew phrase, not a different text, and 1 Kgs 8:12's construal was left out (2026-11-22); but
   there the matter was the same either way, and here it is not. 1554 (canvas 205, column 386, 1200 px: *eumq; super
   mœni-|bus immolauit. Hac tanta atrocitate permoti | Israelitæ, ab eo discedunt*), 1573 (canvas 250, search API: *Hac
   tanta atrocitate per-|moti Israelitæ, ab eo discedunt*), 1738 (`text/orig/2KI.usfm`) alike.
   The form of 2 Sam 12:31 (the Hebrew and its sense, nothing about Castellio's source, which is not certain). Words
   checked with `pipeline latin macron-check` (*Hebraicē* as the settled notes have it; *Isrāēlītās* as the preview).
   **Recommendation (Claude): accept, as near the bar** (leave out if a construal of an obscure phrase is never to be
   noted).
   **Apply:** `2Kgs.3.27` la: `permōtī Isrāēlītæ, ab eō` → `permōtī Isrāēlītæ\f + \fr 3:27 \ft Hebraicē וַיְהִי קֶצֶף־גָּדוֹל עַל־יִשְׂרָאֵל, ‘et facta est īra magna adversus Isrāēlītās’.\f*, ab eō`

## C. Places near the bar (recommended left out)

**Decision:**

2. **5:26** *Scīlicet tempus est accipiendī argentum, accipiendī vestēs, ut emās oleās, vīneās, bālantēs, bovēs, servōs
   aut ancillās?*: Castellio's **G. L. … H.** words *ut emas*, "**so that you may buy** olive groves, vineyards …", taken
   out of the draft with the mark (`../2kgs1-25-draft-fixes.md` §2). The Hebrew lists them as things received
   (הַעֵת לָקַחַת אֶת־הַכֶּסֶף וְלָקַחַת בְּגָדִים וְזֵיתִים וּכְרָמִים, "is it a time to take silver and to take garments, and olive groves and
   vineyards …"); so the NRSVue and the BSB ("Is this the time to accept money and clothing, olive groves and
   vineyards …"), with no note, and the KJV. The Clementine Vulgate has the words (*accepisti vestes ut emas oliveta, et
   vineas*); Brenton's LXX does not (*καὶ νῦν ἔλαβες τὰ ἱμάτια, καὶ ἐλαιῶνας καὶ ἀμπελῶνας*): his *G.* source was not
   found. 1554 (canvas 207, column 389, zoom `z207-5_26.jpg`: *accipiendi ueſtes, G. | L.ut emas H.oleas, uineas,
   balantes, boues, ſer-|uos aut ancillas?*), 1573 (canvas 252, search API: *G.L.vt emas H.oleas*), 1738 and 1750 (the
   candidates file) alike. It changes nothing that happens: the olive groves and slaves are in the verse either way, as
   the price of Gehazi's silver rather than goods he took, and the rebuke is the same. Dropped by the rule of 2026-11-19,
   as 1 Kgs 11:5 and 1:8 (2026-11-22). **Recommendation (Claude): leave out.**
   **Apply (only if accepted against the recommendation):** `2Kgs.5.26` la: `vestēs, ut emās oleās` → `vestēs, ut emās\f + \fr 5:26 \ft ‘ut emās’ Castelliō addit, ut Vulgāta; Hebraicē nōn est.\f* oleās`

3. **6:33** *Eō adhūc cum illīs loquente, ecce, dēscendit ad eum nūntius: quem subsecūtus rēx, ita loquitur: “Cum hoc ā
   Jōvā malum sit, quid amplius ā Jōvā exspectem?”*: *quem subsecūtus rēx*, "**whom the king followed**", is not in the
   Hebrew (וְהִנֵּה הַמַּלְאָךְ יֹרֵד אֵלָיו וַיֹּאמֶר, "and the messenger came down to him, and he said"), the Vulgate or Brenton's LXX.
   Castellio's 1554 annotation says he added it from Josephus (canvas 833, column 362: *Hunc locum ex Ioſepho cõpleui,
   additis his uerbis: Quem ſubſecutus rex*, with Josephus' account; `notes-1554-a.md` §1), and 1738 marks the place with
   an asterisk (reader 03, row 761 *rex, \* ita loquitur*). But **the modern Bibles read the king here too**: the NRSVue
   "the king came down to him" (note "Cn: Heb *messenger*"), the BSB "the messenger came down to him. And the king said".
   The context sets it: Elisha has just said the king is following his messenger (6:32), and 7:2 and 7:17 have the king
   there (*cum rēx ad eum dēscendisset*). A reader comparing a modern Bible is not misled. 1554 (canvas 208, column 391:
   *ecce deſcendit ad eum nuncius, quem | ſubſecutus rex, ita loquitur*), 1573 (canvas 252, search API), 1738 alike.
   **Recommendation (Claude): leave out.**
   **Apply (only if accepted against the recommendation):** `2Kgs.6.33` la: `quem subsecūtus rēx, ita loquitur` → `quem subsecūtus rēx\f + \fr 6:33 \ft ‘quem subsecūtus rēx’ Castelliō ex Jōsēphō addit; Hebraicē nōn est.\f*, ita loquitur`

4. **9:27** *quem persecūtus Jēhūs, etiam ferīrī jussit in currū, in clīvō Gūre, apud Jeblaām. Ille ad Mageddōnem fūgit,
   ibique mortuus est*: kind A by the letter. The Hebrew has Jehu's order only (גַּם־אֹתוֹ הַכֻּהוּ אֶל־הַמֶּרְכָּבָה בְּמַעֲלֵה־גוּר, "Strike him
   too, in the chariot, at the ascent of Gur"); the NRSVue "and they shot him" (note "Syr Vg Compare Gk: Heb lacks"), the
   BSB "So they shot Ahaziah" (note "See Syriac, Vulgate, and LXX"). Castellio keeps the Hebrew's bare order, but the
   next words say Ahaziah fled to Megiddo and died: the shot is implied, as in the Hebrew. A construal the context sets
   right. **Recommendation (Claude): leave out.**

5. **8:16** *Annō autem quīntō Jorāmī Achābī fīliī, Isrāēlītārum rēgis, cum rēgnāsset in Jūdæā Jōsāphātus, Jūdææ rēgnum
   iniit Jorāmus*: kind A by the letter. The Hebrew has וִיהוֹשָׁפָט מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה, "and Jehoshaphat king of Judah", which the
   NRSVue drops (note "Gk Syr: Heb adds *Jehoshaphat being king of Judah*") and the BSB leaves untranslated (no note);
   the KJV "Jehoshaphat being then king of Judah", the Vulgate and LXX keep it. Castellio keeps it, construed as "after
   Jehoshaphat had reigned" (1573, canvas 254, search API, and 1738 *quum regnasset*; 1554 had *regnante in Iudæa
   Iosaphato*, "while he was reigning", canvas 209, column 393: `notes-1554-a.md` §4). A regnal date, no plot: dropped
   by the rule of 2026-11-19. **Recommendation (Claude): leave out** (no Apply line).

6. **10:30** *fore, ut ejus etiam trinepōtēs in Isrāēlītārum soliō sedērent*: the Hebrew בְּנֵי רְבִעִים, "sons of the fourth
   (generation)"; the NRSVue "your sons of the fourth generation", the BSB "four generations of your sons", the Vulgate
   *usque ad quartam generationem*. *Trinepōs* is strictly a great-great-great-great-grandson, one generation more than
   Zechariah (15:8–12) is; Castellio uses it for the line's last king. **The book does not contradict itself**: his own
   15:12 says the same word was fulfilled (1573, canvas 258, search API: *vt eius trinepotes in Israelitarum solio
   ſederent: id quod euenit*). 1554 (canvas 211, column 397) the same. A number of generations, kept consistent:
   dropped by the rule of 2026-11-19. **Recommendation (Claude): leave out** (no Apply line).

## D. 1738's footnotes (recommended: none printed)

**Decision:**

1738 prints 5 lettered footnotes in chapters 1–12 (`%` rows of `data/witnesses/read-1738/2Kgs1-25.tsv`; readers 01, 02,
04). As in Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1–2 Samuel and 1 Kings (`../1kgs1-22-phase2/notes-a.md` §D), the edition prints no
glosses (brief.md: "No marginal notes or commentary"). Three are 1554's margins; the two at 4:13 are not in 1554 and
seem to be 1573's (`notes-1554-a.md` §3). Each, with what it glosses:

- **2:12** *d) columen atque præsidium.* (on *currūs et equitēs*, Elisha's cry: Elijah was Israel's "pillar and
  defence").
- **3:14** *e) administro.* (on *appāreō*, "I stand before" the LORD: "I serve").
- **4:13** *f) Elisæus eius linguæ ignarus, vtebatur interprete famulo.* (on *jussit illī*: Elisha, not knowing her
  language, spoke through his servant).
- **4:13** *g) nihil foris cum quoquam negotii habere.* (on *inter suōs sē continēre*: she has no business with anyone
  outside).
- **9:11** *b) insanus.* (on *limphātus*, "mad").

None marks a departure from the Hebrew. 4:13 *f)* is an explanation of the story, not of the text (the Hebrew has
Elisha speak to Gehazi and Gehazi to her, as Castellio's Latin does). **Recommendation (Claude):** print none.

## E. 1738's asterisks

1738's four asterisks with no footnote in these chapters (3:7 *Iosaphatum \** reader 01, row 221; 4:37 *accidit, \**
reader 02, row 452; 6:25 *columbini \** and 6:33 *rex, \** reader 03, rows 723, 761; 1738's numbering, the same verses on
the KJV's) **each stand at the lemma of one of Castellio's 1554 annotations *IN IIII. LIB. REGVM*** (canvas 833, column
362; `notes-1554-a.md` §2), as in Ruth, 1–2 Samuel and 1 Kings. The edition prints no asterisks. Three of the four are
commentary: the Hebrew idioms of 3:7 ("he went and sent") and 4:37 ("she came and fell"), and the use of dove's dung for
food at 6:25 (Josephus: as salt). The fourth, 6:33, defends his addition from Josephus: §C item 3.
