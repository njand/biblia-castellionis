## The preface (c: commentary, no note)

*HVNC librum à Ioſua non eſſe ſcriptum, uno argumento conuinci poteſt, quod in decimo capite citatur liber Recti, ad
probandam hiſtoriam, de ſole conſiſtere iuſſo: qui liber profectò à Ioſua citari non poteſt, cum ſit poſt tempora
Dauidis ſcriptus. Nam primi Regum capite primo, citatur ex libro Recti, hiſtoria de Dauide. Alterum argumentum eſt, quòd
capite 15. ſcribuntur ea de Axa Calebi filia, quæ poſt Ioſuæ mortem geſta ſunt, ut habetur in Iudicum capite primo. Ex
quibus perſpicuum fit, nec hunc librum à Ioſua, nec librum Recti à Moſe fuiſſe ſcriptum, ſed pòſt, aut certè non ante
tempora Dauidis.*

Castellio argues the book was not written by Joshua: 10:13 cites the book of *Rectī* (Jashar), which also tells of David
(the lament of 2 Sam 1:18; his *primi Regum capite primo* names the first chapter of the first book of Kings, where the
Vulgate's numbering would make it the second, 2 Regum 1, unless he counts otherwise: not checked); and chapter 15 tells
of Achsah, which Judges 1 puts after Joshua's death. Neither book, then, is Joshua's or Moses' own, but from David's
time or later. A view on authorship, like 1738's footnote at Judg 18:31 (*hinc adparet, librum hunc scriptum esse post
captiuitatem*): it changes nothing in the text. No note. Recorded in `docs/editions.md`.

## EX CAP. VI. *Septem tubas buccinas.)* (c: a defence of his Latin, no note)

*Septem tubas buccinas.) יובל adiectiuum eſt, quo tubæ ſonus notatur. Inde Iubile dictum eſt, quòd eo anno tubæ clangore
libertas promulgabatur. Indidem Iubilare uerbum Latinum uidetur deductum. Ego cum melius non haberem, buccinas adiectiuè
dixi. Nam ſi clangentes pro clangorijs (ut ita loquar) dixiſſem, ambiguitate non caruiſſet oratio.* (margin *Iubile.*)

The Hebrew at 6:4 is שׁוֹפְרוֹת הַיּוֹבְלִים, "trumpets of rams' horns" (the NRSVue, the BSB). Castellio takes יובל as a
descriptive word for the trumpet's sound and renders it by *buccinās* used as an adjective. The settled 6:4 has *septem
tubās buccinās*, as the lemma (and 6:6, 6:8, 6:13 the same phrase). A reader of the Latin reads "seven trumpets"; nothing
in the sense is lost. No note.

## EX CAP. XX. *Donec cauſam in concilio dixerit.)* (c: commentary, no note)

*Donec cauſam in concilio dixerit.) Poſtquam primùm in porta ſenatoribus cauſam ſuam ſolus expoſuerit, recipietur ille
quidem, ſed ea conditione, ut ſi ſanguinis uindex ueniat, ille ſe iudicio ſiſtat, & ante id iudicium ſit in urbe tutus.
Quòd ſi uel non ueniet accuſator, uel cauſam non obtinuerit, uiuat illic percuſſor uſq; ad deceſſum pontificis.*

An explanation of the procedure in the city of refuge (20:4–6). The settled 20:6 has *dōnec causam in conciliō dīxerit*,
as the lemma. It agrees with the Hebrew ("until he stands before the congregation for judgment") and needs no note.

## 1738's footnotes are Castellio's 1554 margins

- **7:24** 1554 (canvas 127, column 229): *in ualle Achoris* with a superior *a*, and in the margin *a idem Achor & Achan
  eſt.* 1738 prints *\*) idem Achor et Achan est.* at the foot of leaf 273, column 0, its mark after *Achoris* in 7:24
  (read lines, row 634). It joins the valley's name to the man's, the wordplay of 7:25–26. A gloss, no note.
  (`docs/editions.md` and HUMAN.md had placed it at 7:1; corrected in `docs/editions.md`.)
- **22:34** 1554 (canvas 136, column 247): *Vocarunt autē Rubenitæ & Gadini arā a Ed, quòd ea teſtis eſſet, inter eos
  Iouam eſſe Deum.*, margin *a hoc eſt teſtis.* 1738's *a) hoc est, testis.* (leaf 290). The settled verse already says
  why the altar is *Ēd* (*quod ea testis esset*). A gloss, no note.

**Settled text.** Every lemma matches the settled text (`text/JOS.usfm`): 6:4 *septem tubās buccinās*, 20:6 *dōnec causam
in conciliō dīxerit*, 7:24 *in vallem Āchōris*, 22:34 *āram Ēd, quod ea testis esset*. No (d) item. Two readings of the
1554 text seen on the same pages bear on the notes: 16:10's *G.* … *H.* clause (canvas 132, `josh-notes.md` item 3) and
23:5's past tenses (canvas 136, item 4).
