# 1 Samuel 1–15: footnotes from Castellio's 1554 annotations, reader A

Proposed 2026-11-20, phase 2 of decision 85, following the method of `ruth-notes-1554.md` and `judg-notes-1554.md`.
Every annotation on 1 Samuel 1–15 in the 1554 Basel edition was read on the page and sorted as (a) a deliberate
departure from the Hebrew as read today, (b) a word no dictionary has, (c) commentary (no note), or (d) a place where the
annotation shows the settled text differs from what he meant. Decisions 53 and 56; brief.md, "brief Latin footnotes";
the bar of `ot-notes-process.md` §2–3. Chapters 16–31 are reader B's (the annotations on them begin in the same column).

**Where the annotations are** (MDZ bsb10196330, read 2026-11-20). Canvas 825, after *IN RVTHAM* (left column, page 345),
comes ***IN LIBROS REGVM*** with a pictorial initial *R* and a preface, then *EX CAP. I.* (one annotation) and *EX CAP.
VI.* (its first annotation begins at the foot of the left column, page 345, and ends at the top of the right, page
346), then in the right column (page 346) *EX CAP. VI.* (second), *VIII.*, *XII.*, *XIII.*, *XIIII.*, then reader B's
*XVII.* (two) and *XX.*, which runs on to canvas 826. So **seven annotations on chapters 1–15**. Crops:
`sources/mdz-1554/c825-full.jpg` (full page, cached), and read here at 1600 px: `k825-L-reg.jpg` (page 345, from the
Kings preface to *EX CAP. VI.*), `k825-R1.jpg` … `k825-R4.jpg` (page 346 top to *EX CAP. XVII.*). Hands: a reader has
underlined *Iſraelitis* in the 13:1 annotation and written *Iſraelitaſ* in the margin, and written *Adde:* beside the
17:4 annotation (B's); neither is Castellio's and neither is used.

**The preface** (page 345): *REGVM libri duo priores apud Hebræos Samuelis dicuntur: poſteriores, Regum. Ego Latinorum
titulum retinendum putaui. Nam librum de rebus poſt Samuelem geſtis, Samueli adſcribere abſurdum eſt.* (Why the
edition's 1 Samuel is *Regum liber I*.)

**The 1554 text of 1 Samuel 1–15** is on canvases 152–161: *REGVM LIB. I* begins a third of the way down the right
column of canvas 152 (column 280), after Ruth; chapter 16 begins in the right column of canvas 161 (column 298). Pages
read at 1200 px: `t152.jpg` … `t161.jpg` (`t154`–`t161` fetched here), with zooms `z155-4_21.jpg`, `z155-5_5.jpg`,
`z158-12_14.jpg`, `z158-12_15.jpg`, `z159-13_1.jpg`. Canvas *n* holds columns 2*n*−25 and 2*n*−24 (153 = 281–282, …,
161 = 297–298). Where no zoom is named, a margin was read on the 1200-px page; I could read it there, but not letter by
letter at full size.

**1573** (MDZ bsb10141277), for comparison: canvas 190 (`sources/mdz-1573/z190-1sam4_21.jpg`) and canvas 195
(`t195.jpg`); the MDZ search API for the rest.

**Quotations** follow the page: u/v and i/j as printed, a tilde for a nasal, *s* for ſ, *q;* for the *-que* sign.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 6:8 | 825, 345–346 | *Aureaq; ſimulachra quæ illi,&c.)* | Hebrew כלי, "vessels", which is wider, as σκεῦος; here of images, so not *uaſa* or *utenſilia* | c |
| 6:18 | 825, 346 | *Et ad ingens illud ſaxum.)* | אבל is read; but the stone was named before, and the Septuagint has λίθου: read אבן | c (the NRSVue "the great stone", the BSB "the large rock": both with him) |
| 8:12 | 825, 346 | *Hos tribunos, hos centuriones.)* | Hebrew "captains of thousands and of fifties"; no exact number is meant here, so Latin words; where one is, *ſemicenturiones* may be said: *Audendum eſt enim, cum iubet neceſsitas.* | c (vocabulary; `ot-notes-process.md` §2) |
| 12:17 | 825, 346 | *Tritici meſsis eſt,Iouam inuocabo,& tonabit.)* | thunder at harvest was against nature, since thunder comes of hot and cold meeting, in spring and autumn, in hot lands like Syria | c |
| 13:1 | 825, 346 | *Iniuit autem Saulus regnum,&c.)* | the Hebrew word for word, "a son of a year Saul, when he began to reign, and two years he reigned"; it cannot mean *annum agens*; the place is mutilated; Saul reigned forty years (Acts 13, Josephus) | **a** (`1sam-notes-a.md` item 4) |
| 14:36 | 825, 346 | *Eosq; uſque ad lucem matutinam perimerent.)* | Hebrew *diriperent*, but put for *interficere*, as Homer's ἐναρίζειν | c, with a (d) observation below |

**13:1** in full (page 346, lines 25–45):

> *Iniuit autem Saulus regnum,&c.) Apud Hebreos ſic ad uerbum legitur: Natus annum Saulus, cum regnum iniuit, & duos
> annos regnauit. quod quidam ſic interpretantur: Saulus unum annum rex fuerat, & cum duos annos regnauiſſet, &c. ut hic
> primus numerus ad ſpacium regnandi, non ad Sauli ætatem referendus ſit. Sed hoc pacto Hebræa uerba torquentur, neque
> ſententia ſatis conſtat. Non enim diceret, annum agens, ſiue natus annũ in regnando, pro eo quod eſt, cum iam annum
> regnauiſſet: cum hæc loquendi ratio, natus tot annos, nunquam apud Hebræos, ſicut nec apud Latinos, dicatur, niſi de
> hominis ætate, à natiuitate numerando. Deinde non diceret, Natus annum: ſed, Natus unum annum. Solent enim Hebræi
> numerum exprimere, etiam cum de uno loquuntur. Præterea ſupplenda eſſent nonnulla, id quod locum reddit ſuſpectum.
> Solet autem in his libris, Regum ætas & regnandi tempus initio narrationis rerum ab eis geſtarum poni. ut libri ſecundi
> capite ſecundo, ubi ſic ſcriptum extat: Natus erat annos quadraginta Isboſethus Sauli filius, cum regnum in Iſraelitis
> inijt: duos annos regnauit. Quod idem fit in alijs regibus. Itaque arbitror hoc in loco ſcriptum fuiſſe, quot annos
> natus Saulus regnum inierit, quotq; annos regnauerit: ſed locum eſſe mutilatum. Neq; enim duos tãtum annos regnauit,
> ſed quadraginta, ut habetur Actuum 13. & apud Ioſephum in fine libri ſexti, ubi dicitur, Saulus regnaſſe uiuente
> Samuele, annos octo: mortuo autem, duos & uiginti.*

His text marks the two gaps (canvas 159, column 293, zoom `z159-13_1.jpg`): *INiuit autem Saulus regnum, annũ agens †
| & regnauit in Iſraelitas annos † duos.* So Castellio translated the broken Hebrew as it stands, marked where the
numbers fell out, and said so in the annotation. The NRSVue says the same of the Hebrew; proposed in `1sam-notes-a.md`
(item 4).

**1:3, 8:12**: his classical names for God's title and the officers, which the notes leave to the front matter. **6:8,
6:18, 12:17**: commentary; at 6:18 he reads with the Septuagint as both modern Bibles do. **14:36 (d)**: the lemma ends
*perimerent*, "should kill them", the reading the annotation defends; but his 1554 text has *& uſq; ad lucem ma-|tutinam
deprædarentur, neq; eorũ quenquam | reliquum facerent* (canvas 160, column 295), as 1738 and the preview
(*dēprædārentur*). The annotation was written for a wording the printed text does not carry. The printed word is the
Hebrew's own ("plunder them", the NRSVue and the BSB), and *neque eōrum quemquam reliquum facerent* gives the killing,
so nothing needs changing or noting; for `docs/editions.md`. The 6:18 lemma has *illud*, which the 1554 text lacks
(*& ad ingens ſaxum, in quo repoſita eſt*, canvas 155, column 286). Every other lemma matches the 1554 text and the
preview but for spelling.

## 2. The asterisks of 1738

1738 prints seven asterisks without footnotes in these chapters (`1sam1-31-transcription.md`). Each stands at the lemma
of one of the seven annotations above, in the same order, and no annotation lacks one: 1:3 *bellipotentem \*
venerandum*; 6:8 *simulacra, \* quæ illi*; 6:17 in 1738's numbering (KJV 6:18) *saxum, \* in quo*; 8:12 *centuriones \*
creabit*; 12:17 *tonabit, \* atque pluet*; 13:1 *regnum, \* annum agens*; 14:36 *deprædarentur,\**. With Ruth's 3:16,
eight for eight. The asterisk in 1738 marks the places of Castellio's annotations. At 13:1 1738's single asterisk also
replaces 1554's two daggers.

So **23 of 1738's 24 footnotes in chapters 1–15 are Castellio's 1554 margins**, changed only in spelling (*quum*,
*contemtores*, *ii*) and, at 15:17, with the verse number *21* added. **4:21's is not 1554's**: first found in 1573,
whose printer or editor added it (whether by Castellio's last revision, as 1573's title claims for its text, is not
known; 1556 not checked). Every gloss is on the sense; only 12:15's is textual, and it is decided in `1sam-notes-a.md`
(item 5, recommended left out). The 1554 margins I read showed no other lettered gloss in these chapters that 1738
lacks.

## 4. Castellio's *G.* … *H.* marks in 1554

The marks the 1738 reading found (`1sam1-31-transcription.md`) are printed in **1554**'s text itself, in small
capitals: 2:8 *mittat. G. Qui & pre-|cantibus* … *facit in terris.H.Nã Iouę* (153, 281–282); 5:6 *G.L. enatis muribus
H.* and *G. L. ex quo ingenti clade perturbata ciuitas eſt H.* (155, 285; zoom); 9:1 *Bechorati, | L. filij Sarethi, H.
filij Aphiæ* (156, 288); 11:1 *DEinde G.L.poſt unũ ferè menſem H.fecit* (158, 291); 12:14 *ſequemini, G. non infeſtabit
| uos Ioua. H.* and 12:15 *& uos & ᵃ G. | ueſtrũ regem. H.Ioua* (158, 292; zoom). So they are Castellio's own, from 1554
at the latest. 1554 also prints an *H.* at the head of 5:6 (*ſed tranſcẽdunt. | H. Tum Ioua Azotios*, 155, 285; zoom
`z155-5_5.jpg`) with no *G.* before it in 5:1–5 as read; 1738 does not print it.

## For `docs/editions.md` (not written by this reader)

- 1 Samuel in 1554 (MDZ bsb10196330): the text begins on canvas 152, column 280 (*REGVM LIB. I*); chapters 1–15 on
  canvases 152–161 (columns 280–297); chapter 16 begins in column 298. Lettered marginal glosses throughout (24 in
  chapters 1–15). The annotations at the end of the volume: *IN LIBROS REGVM* on canvas 825 (columns 345–346), with a
  preface explaining why Castellio keeps the Latin title *Regum* for the books the Hebrews call Samuel's; seven
  annotations on chapters 1–15 (1:3, 6:8, 6:18, 8:12, 12:17, 13:1, 14:36).
- 1554 marks the broken numbers of 13:1 with two daggers in the text (*annũ agens † & regnauit in Iſraelitas annos †
  duos*, canvas 159), and its annotation calls the place mutilated (Saul reigned forty years). **1573** keeps the
  daggers (canvas 195) and prints the 12:17 and 13:1 annotations in the book, each as *ANNOTATIO* after its chapter
  (canvas 195, after chapters 12 and 13), as it does Ruth's; its search hits for *diriperent* on canvas 197 suggest the
  14:36 annotation likewise (not seen). 1738 replaces the daggers with one asterisk.
- **1738's asterisks mark Castellio's annotations**: in 1 Samuel 1–15 each of the seven stands at the lemma of one 1554
  annotation, in order, one for each (with Ruth 3:16, eight of eight); 1738 does not print the annotations on those
  pages.
- 1738's footnotes in 1 Samuel 1–15: 23 of 24 are 1554's margins; 4:21 *periit gloria* is first found in **1573**
  (canvas 190, *a perijt gloria.*).
- Castellio's *G.*, *G. L.*, *L.* … *H.* marks in 1 Samuel are in 1554's text (2:8, 5:6, 9:1, 11:1, 12:14, 12:15); 1554
  also prints a stray *H.* at the head of 5:6, which 1738 drops.
- The 14:36 annotation's lemma reads *perimerent* where 1554's own text (and 1738) prints *deprædarentur*; the 6:18
  lemma has *illud*, which the text lacks: the annotations were written against a slightly different wording.
- 1573's chapter 13 has a summary, *In Palæſtinos Ionathan. Iſraelitarum fuga. Sauli temeritas. In eum Samuel. Inermes
  Hebræi.* (canvas 195); 1750 prints the same summary (OCR lines 39243–39245).
- A reader's hand in the MDZ copy of 1554 (canvas 825): *Iſraelitaſ* in the margin of the 13:1 annotation, *Adde:*
  beside the 17:4 annotation.
