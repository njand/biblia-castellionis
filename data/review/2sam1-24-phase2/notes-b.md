## A. Modern Bibles follow the versions (kind A)

None proposed. The places where both the NRSVue and the BSB leave the Hebrew are 15:7, 15:20, 17:3, 17:25, 18:3, 19:26,
21:8 and 24:17. At 15:7 (*quattuor annīs*), 15:20 (*Jōva tibi benignus adsit*), 17:3 (the bride) and 18:3 (*nam tū prō
decem mīllibus nostrum es*) Castellio already reads with them (15:7 and 18:3 by his own annotations, `notes-1554-b.md`
§1; 15:20 and 17:3 by his *G.* marks); 19:26 (*Cum enim asinum īnsternere … cōgitārem*: "saddle me a donkey" against
the Hebrew's "I will saddle myself a donkey") changes nothing, Ziba's deceit being the point in all of them; 17:25
(*Jethræ cujusdam Isrāēlītæ*, the Hebrew; both Bibles "Ishmaelite", with 1 Chr 2:17) is a people's name; 21:8 and
24:17 are items 4 and 5.

## D. 1738's footnotes (recommended: none printed)

**Decision:** none printed (Nathan, 2026-11-21: "Approve all")

1738 prints six lettered footnotes in chapters 13–24 (`%` rows of `data/witnesses/read-1738/2Sam1-24.tsv`, letters *e*–*i*
and *a*). **Four are Castellio's 1554 margins** (18:18, 19:27, 19:43, 21:17), changed only in spelling; 15:7's is a later
digest of his annotation, and 24:1's is not in 1554 (`notes-1554-b.md` §3). As in Joshua, Judges, Ruth and 1 Samuel,
and A's chapters, the edition prints no glosses (brief.md: "No marginal notes or commentary"). Each, with what it
glosses:

- **15:7** *e) Iosephus 4 habet, at in Hebræo 40 sunt.* (*quattuor annīs*): the one textual footnote. It reports what
  the NRSVue and the BSB also do (four years, against the Hebrew's forty, both with notes). Castellio's text and the
  modern Bibles agree, so the reader is not misled; no note.
- **18:18** *f) nam supra cap. 14. dicitur filios tres, et filiam vnam sustulisse.* (*sī forte līberīs prīvārētur*):
  reconciles 18:18 with 14:27. Commentary.
- **19:27** *g) diuina quadam prudentia præditus.* (*quasi dīvīnus quīdam angelus*). An image explained.
- **19:43** *h) decem partes, quod decem tribus erant.* (*decuncem*: Castellio's word for the Hebrew's "ten parts").
  It explains a Latin word a reader can find (*decunx*, ten twelfths); `r6.md` and the phase 1 reader note it.
- **21:17** *i) ne moreretur, quum esset Israelitarum lumen.* (*nē lūmen Isrāēlītārum exstingueret*). An image explained.
- **24:1** *a) quum Deus Abrahamo prolem promisisset innumerabilem, Dauid eam numerare voluit, quasi qui Dei promissum
  refelleret. Vid. 1. Par. 27.* (the census): commentary, with a cross-reference (1 Chr 27:23); not Castellio's in 1554.

None marks a departure that meets the bar. **Recommendation (Claude):** print none.

## E. 1738's asterisks

1738's seven asterisks in these chapters (14:13, 15:7, 18:3, 18:17, 21:6, 23:3, 23:8) **each stand at the lemma of one
of Castellio's seven 1554 annotations on 2 Samuel 13–24**, in the same order, one for each (`notes-1554-b.md` §2): with
A's four, eleven of eleven in the book. The raised mark at 21:6 *Darentur* answers no annotation (the chapter's one is
at *monte*): a stray mark, not an asterisk. The edition prints no asterisks. What each annotation does:

- **14:13** paraphrases the woman of Tekoa's speech: commentary (its lemma reflects an earlier wording of the verse).
- **15:7** defends *quattuor* (Josephus; ארבע for ארבעים): **with the NRSVue and the BSB** ("four years").
- **18:3** reads עַתָּה for אַתָּה (*nam tū prō decem mīllibus nostrum es*): **with the NRSVue and the BSB** ("you are worth ten
  thousand of us").
- **18:17** defends *domum* for "to his tent": vocabulary.
- **21:6** reads בְּהַר for בְּחִיר (*in Jōvæ monte* for "the chosen of the LORD"): **with the NRSVue** ("on the mountain of the
  LORD", note "Cn"), against the BSB and the Hebrew. Not the consensus for a note (`ot-notes-process.md` §3): a place's
  description, and one modern Bible reads as he does.
- **23:3** commentary on *rēctor religiōsus* (Hebrew "ruling in the fear of God"), a messianic reading, and on 23:4–5;
  the sense of the verse is the modern Bibles'.
- **23:8** says he translated the place partly from 1 Chr 11:11, the Hebrew of Samuel being corrupt: hence *Jasobaamus*
  (Chronicles' Jashobeam) and *hastā suā vibrandā* (Chronicles' "he wielded his spear"), with Samuel's 800 (and
  Josephus') and "chief of the three"; the Hebrew's הוּא עֲדִינוֹ הָעֶצְנִי, "Adino the Eznite", is left out as part of the
  corruption (`r7.md`, the transcription notes). The NRSVue calls the verse uncertain ("Gk Vg: Meaning of Heb
  uncertain", twice) and gives "Josheb-basshebeth, a Tahchemonite … he wielded his spear against eight hundred"; the BSB
  the same with the Greek. So he gives the modern Bibles' sense; only the hero's names differ. No note (disputed, and a
  name).
