# 2 Samuel 1–12: footnotes from Castellio's 1554 annotations, reader A

Proposed 2026-11-21, phase 2 of decision 85, by the method of `1sam-notes-1554-a.md` (and `ruth-notes-1554.md`,
`judg-notes-1554.md`). Every 1554 annotation on 2 Samuel 1–12 was read on the page and sorted as (a) a deliberate
departure from the Hebrew as read today, (b) a word no dictionary has, (c) commentary (no note), or (d) a place where
the annotation shows the settled text differs from what he meant. Decisions 53 and 56; brief.md, "brief Latin
footnotes"; the bar of `ot-notes-process.md` §2–3. Chapters 13–24 are reader B's. The notes proposed from this are in
`notes-a.md` (reader A, 2026-11-21).

**5:8** in full (page 347, lines 47–62, and page 348, lines 1–2):

> *CLaudosq; & cæcos Dauidi inuiſos.) Hũc locum mutilũ eſſe patet, tum quòd ſententia curta eſt, tum quòd in
> Paralipomenis capite undecimo, adiectum eſt quod hic deſideratur. Qui ſint autem hi cæci & claudi, magna controuerſia
> eſt. Ego puto ſignificari eos, quos atlantas Græci, telamones Latini appellant: quæ ſigna ſunt uirili figura, mutulos
> aut coronas in ædificijs ſuſtinentia. Atq; hãc eſſe huius loci ſententiam arbitror: Tanta erat arcis altitudo, ut eam
> ſuperari poſſe Iebuſæi negarent: itaq; Dauidi oppugnanti dicerent, tum demum irrupturum eſſe in arcem, cum telamones
> illos, qui cæcorum claudorumq; figuram referentes in ſumma arce erant, abſtuliſſet. quod perinde erat ac ſi dicerent,
> fieri non poſſe ut eò irrumperet. Tum Dauid intelligens, præmijs & honoribus homines ad uirtutem excitari, imperatorem
> fore pronunciauit eum, qui primus Iebuſæos ſuperauiſſet, & ad tecti arcis canales (ubi erant claudi illi & cæci
> telamones) aſcendiſſet, illosq; telamones abſtuliſſet. Atq; eos Dauid propterea oderat, quòd ei ab hoſtibus probroſe
> opponebantur, quaſi claudos & cæcos ſuperare non poſſet, à ſimulachris ad homines per iocũ tranſeunte dicterio, unde &
> natum prouerbium.*

His 1554 text marks the gap with a dagger, as at 1 Sam 13:1 (canvas 173, column 322, zoom `z173-5_8.jpg`): *eoq; die
pronũciauit Dauid, | quiſquis Iebuſæos uiciſſet, & ad canalem per-|tigiſſet, claudosq; & cęcos Dauidi inuiſos † ex | quo
natum eſt dictum illud: Cæcus & claudus | domum ne intrent.* 1738 prints an asterisk in its place (*inuisos, \* ex quo*,
`2sam1-24-transcription-readers.md`, reader 03) and the preview neither (*Dāvīdī invīsōs, ex quō nātum est*). So
Castellio translated the broken sentence as it stands, marked the break, and said in the annotation that the reward
(*imperatorem fore*, which 1 Chr 11:6 gives: "shall be chief and captain") has fallen out here. Proposed in `notes-a.md`
(item 1). His reading of the blind and lame as statues is commentary, and no note gives it.

**7:7**: his text (*ecquod ulli præſidum Iſraelitarum (cui meos Iſraelitas regendos mandauerim) uerbũ feci*, canvas
174, column 324) follows Chronicles' שֹׁפְטֵי, as he says; the Hebrew of Samuel (שִׁבְטֵי) is the one the modern Bibles
smooth into "tribal leaders" (the NRSVue, note "Or *any of the tribes*") and "leaders" (the BSB): no reader is misled.
**12:26**: his *regiam urbis partem* and *urbis partem … in qua erat aquatio* for "the royal city" and "the city of
waters", defended as Latin; the NRSVue "the royal city", "the water city", the BSB "the royal fortress", "the water
supply": no note. **1573** prints the 12:26 annotation in the book (canvas 215: *26 Cum regiam vrbis partem cepiſſet.)
In Hebreo eſt, cùm vrbem regni. …*, search API), as it printed 1 Samuel 12:17 and 13:1's (`1sam-notes-1554-a.md`).

**7:23 (d)**: the lemma reads *in terris*, "on earth", the reading לארץ the annotation commends; but his 1554 text has
*ut eius cauſa tam inaudita & terribi-|lia facerent, quàm tuę terrę feciſti in tuorũ gra-|tiam* (canvas 175, column 325,
zoom `z175-7_23.jpg`), "for your land", the Hebrew's לְאַרְצֶךָ, as 1738 (*quam tu tuæ terræ fecisti*) and the preview
(*quam tū tuæ terræ fēcistī in tuōrum grātiam*). The annotation was written for a wording the printed text does not
carry, as at 1 Sam 14:36. His other reading, להם "for them" for the Hebrew's לָכֶם "for you", is in the text (*ut ejus
causā … facerent*). The Vulgate has both (*faceretque eis magnalia et horribilia super terram*). The modern Bibles leave
the Hebrew here for the Greek's "driving out nations and their gods" (`notes-a.md` item 9); neither *in terris* nor *tuæ
terræ* is their reading, so the slip changes nothing a note would say. For `docs/editions.md`.

**Three are not 1554's** (no letter at the word, no gloss in the margin, on the 1200-px pages and, for 8:18, the zoom):

- **6:23** *o) scilicet ex Dauide. Nam ex Adriele quinque filios habuit. cap. 21, 8.* (on *liberos*): 1554 *Igitur
  Michol Sauli filia nullos per omnẽ uitã liberos habuit.* (174, 324), no margin. The 1573 search finds *ex Adriele*
  alone on canvas 212, where 2 Samuel 6 stands, as a separate line (snippet `ex [Adriele]`), so the gloss is probably
  1573's; the page image was not read.
- **8:18** *p) præcipui, quibus in primis vtebatur.* (on *antistites*): 1554 *Dauidis autem filij erant antiſtites.* (175,
  326; zoom `z175-8_18.jpg`), no margin. Not found in 1573 by the search (its *vtebatur* hits are 1 Chr 18:17's text,
  canvas 277, and others).
- **9:9** *q) Michæ.* (on *filio*, *id domini tui filio do*): 1554 *id domini tui filio do.* (176, 327), no margin. Not
  found in 1573 by the search (its *Michæ* hits are Judges').

Every gloss is on the sense or the names. The textual ones (2:9, 2:29, 4:6) say what the Greek or the Latin read: 2:9
and 2:29 names (Geshurites, Bethhoron, where Castellio's text has *Assuræos* and *Bethrone*, the Hebrew's), 4:6 the
Greek's doorkeeper asleep over the wheat, where the Hebrew is uncertain (the NRSVue "Meaning of Heb uncertain") and the
BSB keeps the Hebrew. None meets the bar (`notes-a.md` §D). The 1554 margins I read showed no other lettered gloss in
these chapters that 1738 lacks.

## 4. Castellio's *G.* … *H.* marks in 1554

The marks the 1738 reading found in chapters 1–12 are printed in **1554**'s text, in small capitals (canvas 171,
column 318; canvas 175, column 326, read on the 1200-px pages):

- 1:19 *Sic autem dixit: G.L.Con-|ſidera, ô Iſraelita, qui in tuis faſtigijs cæſi occu-|buerint. H. Iſraelitarum decus …*
  (the stop after *occubuerint* read on the 1200-px page only, not zoomed; 1738 a comma).
- 1:26 *quàm muliebris | amor. L. Sicut mater unicum amat filiũ ſuum, | ita ego te diligebam. H. Ergo'ne ceciderunt |
  heroes, & arma periere bellica?*
- 8:7 *quas Hieroſolymam | intulit. G. quas poſtea Seſacus Aegypti rex ab-|ſtulit in ea expeditione, quam Roboami So-|lomonis
  filij temporibus fecit in Hieroſoly-|mam. H.*
- 8:8 *ingentem uim æris abſtulit rex Da-|uid, G.L. ex quo fecit Solomo rex lacum æreũ, | & columnas, L. & altare G. &
  lauacra, omniaq; | uaſa. H.*
- 8:13 *Syrorum octodecim | millibus, L. & apud Gebelem ad uiginti tria | millia. H.*

So they are Castellio's own, from 1554 at the latest, as in 1 Samuel.
