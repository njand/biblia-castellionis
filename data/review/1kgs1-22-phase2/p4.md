# 1 Kings 16–22: proofreader p4 (2026-11-22)

I read every verse of 16–22 (250 verses) in `text/draft/1KI.preview.usfm` (rebuilt 13:24 with p1–p3's rows) against the
BSB with its layout, the Hebrew, the Vulgate and 1738 (`text/orig/1KI.usfm`), as p1–p3 did for 1–15.

**Words.** A script compared the preview's words with 1738's. It folded case, macrons, æ/ae, i/j and u/v, and applied
the approved word, stem and phrase rules of `data/rules/orthography.tsv` to 1738's side. Every difference is an
approved rule or a listed draft fix (`1kgs1-22-draft-fixes.md`):
- **Draft fixes:** 16:1 *Hanānis* (*Ananis*); 17:1 *adpāreō* (*adparco*); 17:4, 17:6 *corvīs*; 17:10 *Sarephtham*;
  18:22 Castellio's *G. L. … H.* dropped; 20:14 *prōmittit* (*promitit*); 21:22 *Nabātī*, *Ahiæ*; 22:34 *lōrīcam*;
  22:48 *Asiōngābre*; 22:52 *Jōvamque* (*Iou mque*). 18:17's missing stop after *est* is r7's colon.
- **Rule changes:** *quum → cum*, *audiverunt → audiērunt* 16:16, *audivisse → audīsse* 20:31, *consumtum →
  cōnsūmptum*, *nec ne → necne* 22:6, *simulac → simul ac* 19:13, *adficiam, adfeci → afficiam, affēcī* 16:3,
  *adgressus → aggressus*, *adloquitur → alloquitur*, *adpellabo → appellābō*, *eis → iis*, *conditionem →
  condiciōnem* …

I found no omission or misreading. Every punctuation and capital difference from 1738 is a reader's row with its reason,
or a dropped mark (18:22). No other printer's slip turned up.

**Spelling gaps: words that need a rule.** The preview keeps 1738's spelling in these. The approved rules change
other forms of the same verbs, and the settled text has only the other spelling:
- 17:1 ***adpāreō*** → *appāreō*. 18:15 prints *appāreō* in the same oath. Gaffiot heads *appāreō*, and the rules give
  *adparet, adparuit* …
- 18:24 ***adpellāte*** → *appellāte*. It stands beside the rule-made *appellābō* in the same sentence.
- 17:6 ***adferentibus*** → *afferentibus* (the rules give *adferebant, adferret* …).
- 20:21 ***adficit*** → *afficit* (the rules give *adficio, adficiet, adficitis* …).
- 22:12 ***adgrederētur*** → *aggrederētur*, as p1's 2:29 *adgrediatur*.
- 19:6 ***adspexisset*** → *aspexisset*. Gaffiot heads *aspiciō*, and the rules give *adspexi, adspexerunt* …
- 18:28 ***seipsōs*** → *sē ipsōs* (decision 51). The approved rule covers *seipsum* only. The settled 1 Sam 14:20
  *seipsōs* would need the same change.
- 21:10, 21:13 ***maledīxisse*** → *male dīxisse*? The settled text writes the perfects apart (*male dīxit*, *male
  dīxerit* 9×) and the present forms joined (*maledīcere, maledīcunt* …). This one is for Nathan.

Doubts for Nathan. Gaffiot heads the *ad-* form of these, as p2 found for *adstruō, adliciō*:
- 18:24 *adnuerit*: Gaffiot heads *adnuō*, but the approved *adnuisset → annuisset* assimilates the verb.
- 20:33 *adripientēs*: Gaffiot heads *adripiō*; there is no settled form.
- 22:38 *adluerētur*: Gaffiot heads *adluō*; there is no settled form.
- 19:11 *adsistat*: Gaffiot heads *adsistō*. The settled text has both spellings (*adsistat* Num, *assistit, assistent*).

**Macrons: 25 forms rows** (`p4-forms.tsv`):
- **Copies a reader's row made wrong.** A row without `#` changes every copy of the word in its verse.
  - 17:14#1 ***Jōva***: *prōnūntiat Jōva*, where r7's *ab Jōvā* row made both copies ablative.
  - 19:11#2–#4 ***Jōva***: *ecce Jōva trānsībat*, and twice *Jōva nōn erat*. r8's *cōram Jōvā* row made all four
    copies ablative, though r8's own note reads the other three as nominative.
- **Ablatives:**
  - *Jōvā* at 21:3 (*Jōvā volente*), 21:17 (*ā Jōvā*) and 21:19 (*ā Jōvā*).
  - *gestīsque* 16:5, *compositāque* 18:33 (*compositāque strue*), *eā lēge* 20:39.
  - *audītā* 21:15 and *audītīs* 21:27, both ablative absolutes.
  - *Benadadō* 20:9 (dative) and 20:16 (ablative absolute), as p3's 15:18.
- ***domūs*** 18:3 (genitive) and 20:6 (accusative plural), as the settled *domūs*. ***suaque*** 20:4 (*sē suaque
  omnia*).
- **Perfect subjunctives** take *-erīs*, as p1 and p3 have them: ***flexerīs*** 18:37 and ***occīderīs*** 21:19. The
  second overrides r8's short *-eris*.
- ***hīc***, the adverb: 19:9 and 19:13 (*Quid hīc agis, Ēlīā?*, Hebrew פֹה, here); 21:20 (*Hīc Achābus:*), as r7's
  18:24 and r9's 22:18.
- ***īnfestōs*** 19:2: the edition marks *īnfest-* short throughout (11 settled forms).
- ***Idemque*** 22:12: neuter, "the same thing", as the settled *Idemque*. The preview's *Īdēmque* fits no form.
- ***comminctōrem*** 21:21, as r6's 14:10. Gaffiot marks *mingō, mīnxī, mīnctum*, so both verses may want
  *commīnctōrem*. That needs Nathan's call on 14:10 as well.

I checked every other word in context:
- **Ablatives:** *famē, merīdiē, vērē, cadō, seriā, quādam jūniperō, ūnā … alterā viā, lævāque, eā urbe*.
- **Perfects against presents:** *ēmit* 16:24, *vēnit* 16:31, 17:10, 19:3; *venit* 22:15 (historic present beside
  *interrogat*); *comēdit* 19:6; *lambit* 18:38.
- **Subjunctives and futures:** the 3rd-person *-erit* subjunctives (*antecesserit, dīmīserit*); the future perfects
  *redieris* 22:28, *fuerō, īverō* 18:12.
- ***Hic*** the pronoun at 16:30, 21:25 and 18:37 (*hic populus*).
- **Fourth declension:** *currūs* 22:35, *exercitū, sinū, dēlēctū, reditū, currū*.

`macron-check` on 16–22 flags only settled conventions (*Jōv-, Ēlīam, usque, Quousque, quæsō*) and names as the names
reader set them. Its unknown words are names, æ-forms, *depsitītium* (settled), *obvēlat* (Gaffiot *obvēlō*),
*comminctorem* and *adluerētur*.

**Layout: 2 rows** (`p4-layout.tsv`):
- **19:5:** a comma for 1738's semicolon in *cum … obdormīvisset, ecce aderat angelus*. The semicolon cut the
  *cum*-clause off from its own main clause.
- **22:19:** a paragraph at *Et Michæās: “Ergō accipe Jōvæ sententiam*. 22:13–28 ran to 289 words, so it is split at
  a BSB break where Castellio's sentence begins: 22:13–18 (125 words) and 22:19–28 (164).

The rest is right as the readers set it:
- **Quotation marks:** balanced throughout (checked by script).
  - **Inner ‘…’:** 20:3, 20:5–6, 20:13, 22:11, 22:17, 22:20–22.
  - **Reported speech unmarked:** 17:8–9, 17:10–11, 17:14, 18:1, 18:5, 18:8, 18:10–11, 18:24, 18:33–34, 18:40,
    18:41, 19:2, 19:11, 19:20, 20:4, 20:7–12, 20:18, 20:22–25, 20:28, 20:31, 20:35–37, 21:2–6, 21:13–15, 21:23,
    22:3–4, 22:6, 22:9, 22:12–14, 22:30–34, 22:36.
  - ***inquit* set off:** 17:13, 17:21, 17:23–24, 18:7, 18:18, 18:22, 19:7, 19:15, 20:34, 21:7, 21:19–20, 22:5,
    22:8, 22:18, 22:22, 22:24–26, 22:28.
- **Paragraphs:** every 1738 indent is kept (16:1, 16:6, 16:21, 16:28, 17:1, 17:17, 18:1, 18:6, 19:1, 19:19, 20:1,
  20:22, 20:35, 21:1, 21:8, 22:1). The others open Castellio sentences at BSB breaks. With 22:19 added, the longest
  paragraphs are 19:8–18 (249 words) and 20:1–12 (243), both under the measure.
- **Poetry:** none. 16:4, 21:21–24, 22:17 and 22:36 stay prose where the BSB sets verse (the readers' reasons).

**Left as they stand:**
- 18:3 *prōcūrātōrem, (erat is …*: the comma before the parenthesis is as printed.
- 19:10 *mē solō relictō* against 19:14 *mē ūnō relictō*: Castellio varies the repeated speech, and both are as
  printed.
- 22:6 *necne? cui illī respondent*: r9's lower case after an indirect question.
- 20:14: the prophet's first answer stays narration (r8). 22:16 keeps Castellio's statement where the Hebrew asks a
  question (r9).

Rows: 2 layout, 25 forms.
