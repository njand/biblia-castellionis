# Splitting the longest paragraphs (proposal)

Proposed 2026-11-12 (Claude), at Nathan's request. Nothing is applied until the Decision line is filled in.

The settled text has 2,095 prose paragraphs, with a median of 53 words; 90% are under 154 words. 61 run over 250 words
and 9 over 400: the longest is John 6:22–58 at 612 words. They are mostly in Genesis, John, Acts and Exodus. They come
from the edition's rule of taking the BSB's section breaks and only some of its ordinary breaks (about one in five,
Romans and 1 Corinthians).

**Method.** Every paragraph over 250 words was split where modern editions break and the sense turns.
- Three editions were read for their breaks:
  - **SBLGNT** (Holmes's sense paragraphs; New Testament only), weighted double;
  - the **BSB**;
  - the **WEB**.
- The English editions also break at every change of speaker, so a break was taken only where at least two
  editions agree, or where SBLGNT breaks.
- The verse before the break must end a sentence in the Latin.
- Among those breaks, the one nearest the middle of the paragraph was chosen, again for any half still over 250
  words.
- Each break was then read in the Latin. Breaks in the middle of an exchange were moved to the nearest turn in the
  story (Gen 38:12 and 38:24, 42:6 and 42:17, 44:6, 48:8; Exod 10:12; Matt 25:19; Luke 19:15; John 13:12).
- Left whole:
  - Gen 33:1–17 (301 words): the Latin runs on from 33:11 into 33:12.
  - Gen 44:18–34 (269 words): Judah's single speech.
  - Gen 46:8–27 (266 words): the list of Jacob's household.
- Where the break falls inside a speech that runs on, the new paragraph opens with “, as the settled text already
  does (Matt 5:13).

**64 breaks in 58 paragraphs.** Each item gives the paragraph, the editions that break there,
and the BSB's words at that point.

**Recommendation: accept all.**

**Decision:** accept all (Nathan, 2026-11-12)
