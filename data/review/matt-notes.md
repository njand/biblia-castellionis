Nothing from the NET or the NRSVue is copied here beyond a word or two. "The modern editions" means SBLGNT (2010) and NA28 (2012), against the TR (`nt-notes-form.md`, decision 1).

In every note and bracket below, the NRSVue reads with the modern text against Castellio. Where it keeps his reading as a footnote alternative ("Or …"), the item is left out.

The notes use the formulas of `nt-notes-form.md` §3: *Cōdicēs antīquiōrēs habent / addunt …* where the modern editions differ from the Greek Castellio translated; *Græcē …* where Castellio's Latin says what the Greek does not; brackets for words the modern editions lack, whether the TR has them or not.

## B. Notes where Castellio's Latin says what the Greek does not

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-17: "Apply them")

13. **13:35** *ab Ēsāiā vāte*: TR and the modern editions διὰ τοῦ προφήτου, "through the prophet", with no name. The words quoted are Ps 78:2.
    - Castellio follows the reading with Isaiah's name, which the NET (tc) finds in a few significant manuscripts (א* Θ ƒ1, 13 33) and the NRSVue gives in a footnote. Since the TR has no name either, this is his own choice, so the note takes the *Græcē* form.
    - 1573's OCR has *ab Esaia vate*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.13.35` la: `ab Ēsāiā vāte` → `ab Ēsāiā\f + \fr 13:35 \ft Græcē διὰ τοῦ προφήτου, ‘per vātem’, sine nōmine; verba sunt Psalmī 78:2.\f* vāte`
14. **3:16** *vīdit Jōhannēs*: the Greek (TR and SBLGNT) εἶδε has no subject, and the subject is Jesus (NRSVue "he saw"). Castellio makes John the one who sees, as at Mark 1:10, which you accepted. *Vidit Ioannes / Johannes* in 1573 and in every later printing whose OCR gives the verse (1715, 1726, 1747, 1750, 1758).
    **Apply:** `Matt.3.16` la: `vīdit Jōhannēs Deī` → `vīdit Jōhannēs\f + \fr 3:16 \ft Græcē εἶδε, ‘vīdit’, sine nōmine; Castelliō Jōhannem intellegit.\f* Deī`
15. **11:19** *Estque suīs aliēna sapientia*: Greek (TR) ἐδικαιώθη ἡ σοφία ἀπὸ τῶν τέκνων αὐτῆς; the modern editions ἔργων, "deeds" (NRSVue "Yet wisdom is vindicated by her deeds", with a footnote giving "children").
    - Castellio reads ἐδικαιώθη ἀπό as "is estranged from", so that wisdom is a stranger to her own. The modern reading is the reverse: wisdom is proved right.
    - The NET (tn) gives "shown to be right" as the alternative. Neither the NET nor the NRSVue offers Castellio's sense.
    - *aliena sapientia* in 1556, 1573, 1715, 1747, 1750 and 1758. Luke 7:35 is the same (Luke's file, item 14).
    - The note gives the Greek's plain sense and the older manuscripts' ἔργων; *jūstificāta* (Gaffiot *jūstificō*, `latin macron`: *iūstificāta*) in the edition's j.
    **Apply:** `Matt.11.19` la: `aliēna sapientia.”` → `aliēna sapientia\f + \fr 11:19 \ft Græcē ἐδικαιώθη ἡ σοφία ἀπὸ τῶν τέκνων αὐτῆς, ‘jūstificāta est sapientia ā fīliīs suīs’; cōdicēs antīquiōrēs ἀπὸ τῶν ἔργων αὐτῆς, ‘ab operibus suīs’.\f*.”`
16. **20:5** *septimam*: Greek (TR and SBLGNT) περὶ ἕκτην καὶ ἐννάτην ὥραν, "the sixth and the ninth hour". NRSVue "about noon and about three o'clock"; the Vulgate *sextam*.
    - The parable's hours are the third, sixth, ninth and eleventh.
    - *Septimam* is in 1556 (*ſeptímá*, OCR), 1573 (canvas 766, read on the page), 1681, 1747, 1750 and 1758 (OCR). So it is Castellio's own slip, not one printing's, and it gets a note, not a correction.
    - Not seen: 1551, 1554, and 1715 and 1726, whose OCR doesn't give the verse. So the note says *Castelliōnis ēditiōnēs*, as at 1 John 4:4, not *omnēs*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.20.5` la: `circā septimam et` → `circā septimam\f + \fr 20:5 \ft Græcē περὶ ἕκτην, ‘circā sextam’; Castelliōnis ēditiōnēs ‘septimam’.\f* et`

## D. Corrections to the edited text

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-17: "Apply them")

Each was read on 1573's page image (MDZ, 2026-11-17). Items 39, 41 and 42 are slips that came in from Wikisource: 1573 prints the right word, and `text/orig/MAT.usfm` copied the slip too. For those, the same word in `text/orig/MAT.usfm` would be corrected by hand on acceptance (the Apply lines reach `text/` only), as noted in each item.

39. **5:20** *Hōs* → *Hoc*. 1573 prints *Hoc enim vobis dico* (canvas 752). 1573's OCR, 1681 and 1750 also read *Hoc*; Wikisource and WordPress have *Hos*. *Hōs* makes no sense here. In `text/orig/MAT.usfm`: *Hos enim* → *Hoc enim*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.5.20` la: `Hōs enim vōbīs` → `Hoc enim vōbīs`
40. **10:19** *suggererētur* → *suggerētur*: the Greek has a future, δοθήσεται. 1573 and 1726 print *suggereretur*, a misprint; 1556 (OCR), 1715, 1750 and 1758 print *suggeretur*. Mark 13:11 has *suggerētur* in the settled text. Gaffiot: *suggĕrō*. `text/orig` keeps 1573's misprint (decision 58), as for Mark 5:3.
    **Apply:** `Matt.10.19` la: `suggererētur enim` → `suggerētur enim`
41. **12:45** *sitque* → *fitque*: Greek γίνεται, "becomes". 1573 prints *fitque* (canvas 760), as do 1681, 1715 and 1750; only 1758 and Wikisource have *sitque*. In `text/orig/MAT.usfm`: *sitque* → *fitque*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.12.45` la: `dēgunt, sitque posterior` → `dēgunt, fitque posterior`
42. **13:4** *ferente* → *serente*: Greek ἐν τῷ σπείρειν αὐτόν, "as he sowed"; Luke 8:5 has *quō serente*.
    - 1573 prints *ſerente* (canvas 761), with a long s, the same letter as in *ſed* in the same line.
    - The OCRs read it as f; 1758 has *serente*.
    - In `text/orig/MAT.usfm`: *eo ferente* → *eo serente*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.13.4` la: `eō ferente alia` → `eō serente alia`
43. **16:9** *quod* → *quot*: Greek πόσους κοφίνους, "how many baskets". 16:10 has *quot … sportās*, Mark 8:19 *quot canistra*, and the Vulgate *quot cophinos*. 1573 prints *quod* (canvas 763); 1747 and 1758 *quot*. `text/orig` keeps 1573's *quod*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.16.9` la: `Et quod canistra` → `Et quot canistra`
44. **25:24** *mētās* → *metās*: the verb *metō*, "you reap" (subjunctive, beside *colligās*), not *mēta*. `pipeline latin macron`: *metās* is "metō, 2nd sg. present subjunctive"; *mētās* is "mēta, accusative plural".
    **Apply:** `Matt.25.24` la: `quī et mētās ubi` → `quī et metās ubi`
45. **4:24** a comma: *lūnāticī membrīs captī* → *lūnāticī, membrīs captī*, three groups as in the Greek (δαιμονιζομένους καὶ σεληνιαζομένους καὶ παραλυτικούς). 1573's OCR has *lunatici, mébris capti*.
    **Apply:** `Matt.4.24` la: `lūnāticī membrīs captī` → `lūnāticī, membrīs captī`

## Considered and left out

**From the apparatus and the TR-alone list.** None of these is a change a reader needs to be told about:
- 5:27 *antīquīs*: the same words stand in 5:21 and 5:33.
- 6:15 *sua dēlicta*: NA28 omits τὰ παραπτώματα αὐτῶν, which the old test (with WH) had kept out; the sense is the same without it.
- 15:6 *præceptum*: NRSVue gives "law or commandment" in its footnote.
- 18:35 *dēlicta*.
- 24:42 *hōrā* for "day": NRSVue gives "at what hour" in its footnote.
- 1:5, 1:7, 1:10, 28:1: the spelling of names (Asa/Asaph, Amon/Amos, Mary).
- 19:9 *et quī repudiātam dūxerit*: SBLGNT keeps it, so it fails the consensus test, although NA28 omits it.
- The single-word entries: tense, compound verbs, "Jesus" supplied, conjunctions.

**From the reading of each chapter.** In each, a modern Bible keeps Castellio's sense as an alternative, or the difference is a shade:
- 5:12 *vātēs majōrēs vestrōs*: ambiguous Latin, not a different sense.
- 5:19 *licet ita doceat*.
- 6:27 *ūnum cubitum addere suæ statūræ*: NRSVue gives "add one cubit to your height" in its footnote. Luke 12:25 is the same.
- 12:36 *malum verbum* for "careless".
- 15:5 the gift to God: Castellio and the Vulgate make it benefit the parent. Left out, as at Mark 7:11.
- 18:22 *septuāgiēs septiēs*: NRSVue gives "seventy times seven" in its footnote.
- 21:7 *super eā*.
- 23:4 *colligent*: 1573 prints it so.
- 26:45 a command where the NRSVue has a question.
- 26:50 *quōrsum ades?*: NRSVue gives "Why are you here?" in its footnote.
- 26:64 no "from now on".
- 27:9 *licitātī ēmērunt*: the Greek is hard, and the NRSVue itself has two footnotes.
- 27:63 *intrā trēs diēs*: left out as Mark 9:31 and 10:34.
- 23:36 and 24:34 *nātiō* and *ætās* for γενεά: his usual words.
