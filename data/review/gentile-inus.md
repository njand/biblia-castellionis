# Gentile adjectives in -īnus (Exod 6:14 *Rūbēnīnæ*)

Proposed 2026-11-16, from the research for Numbers (`data/review/num1-36-stages3-5.md`). Nathan, 2026-11-16: "I think
-īnus should be the standard. Can you find any exceptions to that rule in Gaffiot? If not, be sure to correct any
existing -inus endings."

**What Gaffiot shows** (`python -m pipeline latin macron`; only quantities cited): adjectives of peoples and places
are -īnus (*Tărentīnus*, *Latīnus*, *Sabīnus*; the settled *Palæstīnus*, *Alexandrīnus*, *Ephesīnus* agree). The
exceptions are of two kinds, and the settled text already marks every one of them short: adjectives of material from
Greek -ινος (*cĕdrĭnus*, *byssĭnus*, *coccĭnus*, *hyăcinthĭnus*, *ŏnychĭnus*, *tĕrĕbinthĭnus*, *thyĭnus*; *murrĭnus* in
L&S), and the time adjectives *crastĭnus*, *prīstĭnus*. (Gaffiot marks some material adjectives long too:
*sapphīrīnus*, *cĭlĭcīnus*, *cămēlīnus*.) The Hebrew tribal and clan adjectives (*Rūbēnīnus*, *Gadīnus*,
*Cahathīnus* …) are gentile adjectives, which no dictionary has: -īnus, as Nathan asked.

In the settled books one form is out of line:

1. **Exod 6:14** *Rūbēninæ* → *Rūbēnīnæ*.
    **Apply:** `Exod.6.14` la: `hinc Rūbēninæ cognātiōnēs` → `hinc Rūbēnīnæ cognātiōnēs`

Left as it is, for Nathan: Gen 30:37 *castanīnīs* (Castellio's coinage for 'of the chestnut'; Gaffiot has only
*castănĕus*, and nothing marks *castaninus*; by Gaffiot's tree adjectives from Greek it could be short).

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-16: "be sure to correct any existing -inus endings")
