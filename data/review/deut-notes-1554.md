# Deuteronomy: footnotes from Castellio's 1554 annotations (4)

Proposed 2026-11-18, by the method used for Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus and Numbers (`data/review/gen-exod-notes-1554.md`,
`lev-notes-1554.md`, `num-notes-1554.md`): every annotation on Deuteronomy in the 1554 Basel edition was read on the
page and sorted into (a) deliberate departures from the Hebrew as read today, (b) words no dictionary has that
Castellio explains himself, (c) commentary or defences of Latin a reader can follow (no note), and (d) places where
the annotation shows the settled text (1738) differs from what he meant. Decisions 53 and 56; brief.md, "brief Latin
footnotes"; the bar of `ot-notes-process.md` §2–3 (kind B: the NRSVue, the BSB and the Hebrew agree against him, and
the NRSVue does not call the Hebrew uncertain). The note sits right after its word; the Hebrew is the WLC's
(`sources/parallel/wlc`, Hebrew numbering: KJV 23:18 is its 23:19) without the cantillation marks.

**Where the annotations are** (MDZ bsb10196330; images `https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb10196330_00821/…`,
read 2026-11-18): *In Deuteronomium* begins at the lower right column of canvas 821 (column 338, after the Num 34:6
note) and fills canvas 822 (columns 339–340) and canvas 823 (columns 341–342); it ends two thirds of the way down the
left column of canvas 824 (column 343), where *In Iosuam* begins. Fourteen annotations with a lemma, under the heads
*EX CAP. II., III., VI., VIII., X., XVIII., XX., XXII., XXIII., XXIX., XXXII.*, and then, under *EX CAP. XXXIII.*, a
running paraphrase of the whole Blessing of Moses (33:2–29) with Castellio's defences of single words inside it (16
parts in the table at the end). Two heads do not match the verse: the 7:10 note stands under *EX CAP. VIII.*, and
*In eius loci curiam* (21:19) under *EX CAP. XX.* Crops read: `c821-deut.jpg`, `c822-L1.jpg` … `c822-L8.jpg`,
`c822-R1.jpg` … `c822-R7.jpg`, `c823-L1.jpg` … `c823-L7.jpg`, `c823-R1.jpg` … `c823-R7.jpg`, `c824-L1.jpg` …
`c824-L6.jpg`, zooms `z822-3-8.jpg`, `z823-heb.jpg`, and the 1554 text of 2:7 `c104-deut2-7.jpg`, in
`sources/mdz-1554/`. (The copy has a reader's handwriting: *Adde* after the 7:10 note; references in the margin of 33:2,

**Quotations** follow the page: u/v and i/j as printed, the tilde for a nasal (*tutũ*), *s* for ſ, *ß* written *ss*.
Hebrew in the annotations is given as printed, unpointed. **Modern renderings**: the NRSVue on Bible Gateway (read
2026-11-18, with its footnotes), the NET on Bible Gateway for 23:18, the BSB from `sources/parallel/bsb/Deut.tsv`.

**Settled text.** The settled text (1738) still has Castellio's reading at all four places proposed. One lemma does not
match it: **2:7** *Qui uobis iter tutũ prębuisset* is the 1554 text (canvas 104, *qui uobis iter tu-tum præbuisset in
uasta illa solitudine*), but 1738 (`text/DEU.usfm` and `text/orig/DEU.usfm`) reads *quī vestrum iter cūrāvisset*,
which is nearer the Hebrew יָדַע לֶכְתְּךָ that the annotation defends departing from. So a later printing (Castellio's
own 1556 revision, or a later editor: not checked) revised the verse; the settled text is the closer one and nothing
needs correcting (not a (d) correction; recorded for `docs/editions.md`). The other lemmas match, with spelling
differences only (22:9 *redditum*, settled *reditum*; 23:18 *precium*, settled *pretium*).

## 1. Deut 7:10 *sine dubiō*

The Hebrew לֹא יְאַחֵר לְשֹׂנְאוֹ אֶל־פָּנָיו יְשַׁלֶּם־לוֹ is "he will not delay; he will repay him to his face" (NRSVue "He
does not delay but repays in their own person those who reject him"; BSB "He will not hesitate to repay to his face
the one who hates Him"; no footnote in either). Castellio (canvas 822, column 339, under *EX CAP. VIII.*): *Eiq; sine
dubio corãm digna factis præmia rependentem.) Ab alijs ita uertitur hic locus, ut dicatur Deus improbos statim
ulcisci, sine cunctatione. Sed id à Dei natura est alienum, qui est ad iram tardus: & res ipsa clamat, improbos non
statim puniri: id quod cùm alij multi, tum Ieremias conqueritur. Præterea uerbum אחר non propriè cũctari significat,
sed id quod ὑστερεῖν dici potest: Latinè exprimitur duabus negationibus, hoc modo: Si uotum feceris, noli non
reddere, id est, redde sine dubio, ut est Deuter. 23. Et Habacuc 2 ita legitur: Si cunctabitur, expecta eum, ueniet
enim לא יאחר, id est sine dubio. sed Gallicè bene exprimitur, Et ny faudra point. Quod si ita redderetur, Veniet enim,
non cunctabitur: contrarium esset ei quod antè positum est, Si cunctabitur, expecta eum.* So he understood the
Hebrew's "not delay" and set it aside on theological grounds (God is slow to anger) and by his reading of אחר ("fail,
fall short"); the settled text has *iīsque sine dubiō cōram digna factīs præmia rependentem*.

Modern renderings keep the time: "does not delay", "will not hesitate", "will not be slow" (NIV). Nobody calls the
verse uncertain. The reader comparing a modern Bible finds "without fail" where the Hebrew says "without delay", and
Castellio's change is exactly the point the modern versions keep.

> *Hebraicē לֹא יְאַחֵר, ‘nōn cūnctābitur’; Castelliō, ratus Deum, quī ad īram tardus est, improbōs nōn statim ulcīscī, ‘sine dubiō’ vertit.*

("Hebrew 'he will not delay'; Castellio, holding that God, who is slow to anger, does not punish the wicked at once,
renders 'without fail'.") *Ad īram tardus* and *nōn statim ulcīscī* are his own words. Words checked with `pipeline
latin macron` (cūnctābitur, ulcīscī, tardus). Shorter, if preferred: *Hebraicē לֹא יְאַחֵר, ‘nōn cūnctābitur’.*

**Recommendation (Claude):** leave it out. "Without fail" keeps that God repays the one who hates him; only the timing
goes, on purpose and argued at length. If you want it, the shorter wording, since the notes no longer give Castellio's
reasons.

**Decision:** reject (Nathan, 2026-11-18: "As recommended")
**Apply:** `Deut.7.10` la: `iīsque sine dubiō cōram` → `iīsque sine dubiō\f + \fr 7:10 \ft Hebraicē לֹא יְאַחֵר, ‘nōn cūnctābitur’; Castelliō, ratus Deum, quī ad īram tardus est, improbōs nōn statim ulcīscī, ‘sine dubiō’ vertit.\f* cōram`

## 2. Deut 10:8 *et ejus nōmen celebrandum*

The Hebrew וּלְבָרֵךְ בִּשְׁמוֹ is "and to bless in his name" (NRSVue "to bless in his name"; BSB "to pronounce blessings in
His name"), the priests' blessing of the people pronounced in God's name (Num 6:23–27; the same phrase in Deut 21:5).
Castellio (canvas 822, columns 339–340, *EX CAP. X.*, margin *Celebrare.*): *Et eius nomen celebrandum.) ברך & bene
precari, & celebrare significat. Sed si hic bene precari significaret, primùm curta esset oratio. Deinde cum Leuitici
muneris pars sit uel præcipua Deum laudare, ut est in Paral. 16. non est consentaneũ, hoc in loco (in quo nominatim
de Leuitarum munere tractatur) nullã huius rei factam esse mentionem. Hæc causa fuit, ut ita transtulerim. Atq; in
eandem sententiam transferunt etiam alij, 1. Par. 23.* So he chose "praise his name" over "bless (the people) in his
name" on purpose, because a blessing would want an object and the Levites' chief task is praise; the settled text
has *et ejus nōmen celebrandum*.

Modern renderings agree on blessing in his name; the BSB's "pronounce blessings" makes the people the object, and
the NRSVue gives no footnote. A reader comparing a modern Bible finds the Levites praising God where the Hebrew has
them blessing in his name. The weaker of the four: the NRSVue's "to bless in his name" could be read either way, and
the difference is one of what the blessing is, not of a word changed.

> *Hebraicē וּלְבָרֵךְ בִּשְׁמוֹ, ‘et ad benedīcendum in nōmine ejus’, quod hodiē dē benedictiōne sacerdōtum intellegitur; Castelliō Deī laudem intellegit.*

("Hebrew 'and to bless in his name', which is now understood of the priests' blessing; Castellio understands the
praise of God.") Words checked (benedīcendum, laudem, intellegitur). Shorter: *Hebraicē וּלְבָרֵךְ בִּשְׁמוֹ, ‘et ad
benedīcendum in nōmine ejus’.*

**Recommendation (Claude):** leave it out: the NRSVue's "to bless in his name" reads either way, so the reader is not
clearly misled.

**Decision:** reject (Nathan, 2026-11-18: "As recommended")
**Apply:** `Deut.10.8` la: `et ejus nōmen celebrandum,` → `et ejus nōmen celebrandum\f + \fr 10:8 \ft Hebraicē וּלְבָרֵךְ בִּשְׁמוֹ, ‘et ad benedīcendum in nōmine ejus’, quod hodiē dē benedictiōne sacerdōtum intellegitur; Castelliō Deī laudem intellegit.\f*,`

## 3. Deut 23:18 *admissūræ canis pretium*

The Hebrew וּמְחִיר כֶּלֶב (WLC 23:19) is "and the price of a dog". The NRSVue keeps it literally ("the wages of a dog",
no footnote); the BSB reads a male prostitute ("the wages of a prostitute, whether female or male"), as do the NET
("the wage of a male prostitute", note: *Heb "of a dog." This is the common Hebrew term for a noncultic … male
prostitute*) and the NIV. Castellio (canvas 822, column 340, *EX CAP. XXIII.*): *Aut admissuræ canis precium.)
Iosephi interpretationem sequutus sum, qui uertit ἐπ' ὀχεύσει κυνός. alioquin ad uerbum legitur, canis precium.* (The
Greek as I read it, printed with ligatures; not checked against a Josephus edition.) So he knew the literal "price of
a dog" and followed Josephus's "for a dog's mating", a stud fee; the settled text has *aut admissūræ canis pretium*.

No modern version surveyed has a stud fee: the reader comparing a modern Bible finds either the literal "dog" or a
male prostitute, and Castellio's "covering of a dog" is a reading taken from Josephus, not from the Hebrew (kind C,
*Castelliō … sequitur*).

> *Hebraicē מְחִיר כֶּלֶב, ‘pretium canis’, quō nōmine hodiē plērīque scortum virīle intellegunt; Castelliō Jōsēphum sequitur.*

("Hebrew 'the price of a dog', by which most now understand a male prostitute; Castellio follows Josephus.") Words
checked (scortum, virīle; *scortum* is of either sex in Whitaker's). *Jōsēphum* as the settled text spells the
patriarch (*Jōsēphō*, 33:13); the historian's name has the same form. *Plērīque* rests on the BSB, NET and NIV against
the NRSVue's literal rendering; *multī* would be safer if Nathan prefers.

**Recommendation (Claude):** leave it out: the NRSVue and the BSB disagree (a dog, a male prostitute), so the passage
counts as disputed (`deut-notes.md` §D).

**Decision:** reject (Nathan, 2026-11-18: "As recommended")
**Apply:** `Deut.23.18` la: `aut admissūræ canis pretium in` → `aut admissūræ canis pretium\f + \fr 23:18 \ft Hebraicē מְחִיר כֶּלֶב, ‘pretium canis’, quō nōmine hodiē plērīque scortum virīle intellegunt; Castelliō Jōsēphum sequitur.\f* in`

## 4. Deut 33:8 *quem lacessīstī apud Massam*

The Hebrew תֻּמֶּיךָ וְאוּרֶיךָ לְאִישׁ חֲסִידֶךָ אֲשֶׁר נִסִּיתוֹ בְּמַסָּה תְּרִיבֵהוּ עַל־מֵי מְרִיבָה is read today as spoken to
God about Levi: "Give to Levi your Thummim and your Urim to your loyal one, whom you tested at Massah, with whom you
contended at the waters of Meribah" (NRSVue; the BSB the same, "whom You tested at Massah"). Both add "Give to Levi"
from the Qumran text and the Greek (NRSVue "Q ms Gk: MT lacks *Give to Levi*"), which does not touch this point.
Castellio (canvas 823, column 342, inside the *EX CAP. XXXIII.* paraphrase) reads it as spoken to Levi, with God as
the "man" whom Levi provoked: *De Leui sic loquitur: Tua integritas & claritas (alludit ad hæc uerba, quæ erant in
Pontificis monili inscripta) id est, tuorum morum probitas, & doctrinæ præstantia, quibus ambobus debes hominibus
prælucere, est uiri clementis tui, id est Iouæ: qui tibi, qua est clementia, præcipuè fauet, sacerdotium indulgendo:
quem tu lacessiuisti apud Massam, cum quo iurgasti ad aquam iurgij (nam Moses & Aharon, qui erant Leuitæ, Deo fidem
nõ habuerunt, cum aquam ex petra elicuerũt. cum enim deberent eius rei gloriam Deo tribuere, in eo peccarunt, quòd
dixerunt: Attendite rebelles, an uobis aquam ex hac petra eliciamus. Debebant enim loqui ut Paulus, qui dicit: Non
ego, sed in me Christus) qui uir clemens tuus, id est Ioua Christus, pluris facit obedientiam, quàm cognationem.* The
settled text follows him: *Tua integritās et clāritās est tuī virī clēmentis, quem lacessīstī apud Massam, cum quō
jūrgāstī ad aquam jūrgiī*.

So tester and tested change places: in the modern versions God tests Levi; in Castellio Levi (Moses and Aaron)
provokes God, and the "loyal one" is God. The NRSVue and the BSB agree with each other and with the Hebrew's words;
neither calls the Hebrew uncertain here (the NRSVue's footnote is only on the added words). Kind B.

> *Hebraicē אֲשֶׁר נִסִּיתוֹ בְּמַסָּה, ‘quem tentāvistī in Massā’, quod hodiē Deō dictum intellegitur, quī Lēvīn tentāverit; Castelliō Lēvī dictum, quī Deum lacessīverit, ‘virum’que ‘clēmentem’ Jōvam esse intellegit.*

("Hebrew 'whom you tested at Massah', which is now understood as said to God, who tested Levi; Castellio understands
it as said to Levi, who provoked God, and the 'merciful man' to be the Lord.") Words checked (tentāvistī,
lacessīverint for *lacessīverit*); *Lēvīn*, *Lēvī* as Greek-declined forms of the settled text's *Lēvim* (33:8 *Ad
Lēvim*): Nathan may prefer *Lēvim* / *Lēvī*. Shorter: *Hebraicē אֲשֶׁר נִסִּיתוֹ בְּמַסָּה, ‘quem tentāvistī in Massā’,
Deō dictum; Castelliō Lēvī dictum intellegit.* **Coordinate** with `deut-notes-candidates.md`, which lists 33:8 for the
BSB's "Give to Levi" (DSS and LXX); if a note on that is also accepted, the two should be one note.

**Recommendation (Claude, reviewing this file with `deut-notes.md`):** accept, in the shorter wording now in the Apply
line (the notes no longer give Castellio's reasons, `notes-audit.md`). Tester and tested change places, and the NRSVue
and the BSB agree with the Hebrew's words. "Give to Levi" needs no note (`deut-notes.md` item 7).

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-18: "As recommended")
**Apply:** `Deut.33.8` la: `quem lacessīstī apud Massam,` → `quem lacessīstī apud Massam\f + \fr 33:8 \ft Hebraicē אֲשֶׁר נִסִּיתוֹ בְּמַסָּה, ‘quem tentāvistī in Massā’, Deō dictum; Castelliō Lēvī dictum intellegit.\f*,`

## Not proposed

**Deut 3:8** *terram … Cisjordānīnam*, looked at closely. בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן is "beyond the Jordan" (NRSVue "the land
beyond the Jordan"; BSB "across the Jordan"; NIV "east of the Jordan"). Castellio (canvas 822, column 339, *EX CAP.
III.*): *A' duobus Amorræorum regibus terrã abstulimus cisiordaninã.) בעבר significat trans. Sed cum sit Moses in ea
terra, de qua loquitur, non dubium est, quin eam uocet cisiordaninam … Quare necesse est, aut ut idem uerbum utrunq;
significet: aut בעבר mendum sit, pro מעבר, quod citra significat, nisi cum additur ההלאה, tunc enim significat trans.
Idem uerbum sæpe ambiguum est in Iosua, & Deuteronom. 5. Ego sensum sequor.* (5 as printed.) He keeps the land the
Hebrew means, east of the Jordan, and names it from where Moses stands; the settled text does the same at 4:47 and
4:49 (*Amorræī Cisjordānīnī*, *campum Cisjordānīnum orientālem*). A rendering that keeps the place (the KJV says "on
this side Jordan"; the NIV "east of the Jordan"): no note. (His argument touches the old question of who wrote
Deuteronomy, which may interest the front matter.) Draft, if wanted: *Hebraicē בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן, ‘trāns Jordānem’;
Castelliō, Mōse in eā terrā loquente, ‘Cisjordānīnam’ vertit.*

**Deut 8:7** *lacubus*. תְהֹמֹת is "deeps" (NRSVue "underground waters welling up", BSB "springs"). Castellio (canvas 822,
column 339, margin *Profunda.*): *Quod uerto Lacubus, in Hebræo est profundis. Vocat autem profunda quiescentes aquas,
ut sunt stagna & lacus, quos uidemus in Euangelio maria Hebraico more dici.* A word for a kind of water, where the
modern versions themselves vary (springs, fountains, deeps, underground waters): as Num 13:23 *fluvium*, no note.

**Deut 2:7** *quī vestrum iter cūrāvisset*: his 1554 *qui uobis iter tutum præbuisset* was a sense rendering of יָדַע
לֶכְתְּךָ (*In Hebræo est, Qui uestrum iter nouisset. Sed ego sensum, non uerba reddidi*); 1738 has a reading nearer the
Hebrew, and the BSB's "He has watched over your journey" matches it. No note (see "Settled text" above).

**Deut 33:2** *vēnitque ob sacrās legiōnēs, ignem disciplīnæ iīs habēns*: he takes מֵרִבְבֹת קֹדֶשׁ as "for the sake of
the holy myriads" (Israel), defending מן as *ob* at length, and אשדת as *ignem disciplinæ*. The NRSVue emends both and
footnotes "Meaning of Heb uncertain": disputed, no note.

**Deut 33:5** *inter probōs rēx*: he renders ישרון by its meaning (*singularis pro plurali … significat rectos, æquos,
& … probos: id est Israelitas, ut etiam Esa. 44. appellatur*) and takes the king to be Moses. The name's meaning is
standard, and "he was king in Jeshurun" is read of the Lord by most (BSB) and of Moses by some: no note.

**Deut 33:28** *adjiciet oculum Jācōbus*: עֵין is both "eye" and "spring"; he takes "eye" (*Videre pro potiri*), the BSB
"the fountain of Jacob", the NRSVue "Jacob's abode" (an emendation). A homonym in a disputed line: no note.
