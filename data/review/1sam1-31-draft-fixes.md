# 1 Samuel 1–31: 1738's slips and Castellio's marks taken out of the draft

*2026-11-20, phase 2. The draft (`text/draft/1SA.usfm`) was built from the read lines, which follow the page exactly, so
1738's printing slips and Castellio's *G. … H.* / *L. … H.* marks came through into the edition's starting text. The
printed text (`text/orig/1SA.usfm`) keeps both, as decision 58 requires; the edition reads the words Castellio meant and
drops the marks, keeping their words (as the reading edition does: `gen1-50-orig.md` §4; Josh 16:10 settled so). Whether a
marked passage gets a footnote is the notes pass's (`1sam-notes.md`). Each slip was read on the page in phase 1
(`1sam1-31-transcription.md` and its readers' notes).*

**Applied to the draft** under decision 30's standing rule.

## 3. A reading restored from Castellio's lifetime edition (1)

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-20: "Yes, settle it", on the phase 2 summary `1sam1-31-edition.md` point 4); the printed
text keeps 1738's *improbe*.

1. **20:30** *ō improbe* → *ō improbæ*: Saul's insult is *O' improbæ, inquit, contumacisq; matris progenies* ("son of a
   wicked, contumacious mother") in 1554 (canvas 165, column 305, read by Claude on the zoomed page `n165-20_30.jpg`)
   and 1573 (canvas 201, notes reader B, search snippet); Castellio's own margin there, which 1738 prints as footnote l),
   explains it: *Saulus improbæ filium vocat improbum: sed a matre*. 1738, 1750 and Wikisource print *improbe*, a
   vocative that the margin's *improbæ filium* contradicts: a later printer's change (which printing made it is not
   checked). Found by notes reader B (`1sam-notes-1554-b.md`).
    **Apply:** `1Sam.20.30` la: `o improbe, inquit` → `o improbae, inquit`
