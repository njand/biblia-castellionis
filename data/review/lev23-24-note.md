# Lev 23:24 *monētum*: a footnote on Castellio's coinage (1)

Proposed 2026-11-16. Question 4 of `data/review/lev1-27-stages3-5.md` kept *sabbatum monētum clangōris*: no classical
author has the adjective; Castellio coined it, and his 1554 annotations say so.

**The evidence, read on the page** (1554, MDZ bsb10196330, canvas 820, the annotations *In Leviticum*, under *EX CAP.
XXIII.*): *Sabbathum monetum.) Hoc adiectiuum à mo-|nendo deduxi, quomodo Latini Iunonem Monetã dixerunt.* ("I
derived this adjective from *monēre*, as the Latins called Juno *Monēta*.")

**The note's purpose** (Nathan, 2026-11-16): to tell the reader the word is Castellio's coinage, formed by analogy with
Juno Moneta; no Hebrew is needed. A new kind of note beside decisions 53 and 56 (departures from the Greek or Hebrew):
kept to words no dictionary has that Castellio explains himself (in Leviticus, this one only). Wording: version 1 of
four drafts, after *monētum*:

> *‘Monētum’ Castelliō ā monendō fīnxit, ut Jūnō Monēta.* ("Castellio formed *monētum* from *monēre*, like Juno
> Moneta.")
