# Three spellings from Nathan's reading of the PDF: *cēn-*, *simul ac*, *Hallēlūia*

*2026-11-20.* Nathan, reading the PDF: "Acts 9:39 *cœnāculum*"; "Galatas 1:15 *simulac*: I think we should split
*simul ac*, based on corpus evidence and Gaffiot"; "Apocalypsis 19:1 *Hallēluja*: Gaffiot gives *hallēlūia*."

## A. *cēna* and its family, for *cœna*

**The evidence.** `python -m pipeline latin macron`: *cēnāculum* is Gaffiot's headword (*cēnācŭlum, ī*); *cœnāculum*:
"no source knows this form". *obscēna*: Morpheus and Wiktionary. `latin attest --count`: *cenaculum* 21 hits (Varro,
Livy, Quintilian, Petronius, the Vulgate), *coenaculum* 5, all tier 4 (Isidore, Vives, Buchanan); *cena* 218 (tier 1:
31; Plautus, Martial, Cicero, Horace), *coena* 106, 72 of them Neo-Latin (Erasmus 46). The edition already spells
*cēnāculum* at Mark 14:15, Luke 22:12 and Judg 3:20–24, *cēnam* at Gen 19:3, *obscēn-* at Exod 20:26, 2 Pet 2:10 and in
the front matter; *cœn-* came through from Wikisource and the 1738 lines, which the approved *oe → œ* rule left alone.

**The rules** (`data/rules/orthography.tsv`), approved: the stem rules *cœna- → cena-* (every form of *cēna*, *cēnāculum*,
*cēnātiō* and the verb's *cēnā-* forms; not *coen-* alone, which would take *cœnum* "mud" too) and *obscœn- → obscēn-*.

**The settled text**, thirteen words; the printed text (`text/orig/`) keeps *cœn-* as printed (decision 58).

## B. *simul ac*, for *simulac*

**The evidence.** `latin attest --count`: *simul ac* 210 hits in 140 works by 63 authors (tier 1: 13; Cicero 41,
Lucretius 10, Tacitus 10, Suetonius 9, Livy 7); *simulac* 23 in 19 works (tier 1: 1; Spinoza 6, Nepos 3). Gaffiot (via
`latin macron`) has both *sĭmŭlac* and *sĭmŭl*. The edition already writes *simul ac* 18 times, *simulac* 5.

**The rule**: word, approved, *simulac → simul ac*.

## C. *Hallēlūia*, for *Hallēluja*

**The evidence.** `latin macron hallēlūia`: Wiktionary *hallēlūia* (alternative form of *allēlūia*); Gaffiot, as the
tool reports it, *hallĕlūia · allēlūia*: both mark the *ū* long, which the edition lacked; the tool shows a breve on
the first *e* of the *h-* form, while Gaffiot's *allēlūia* and Wiktionary mark it long (Greek ἀλληλούϊα, η). *ē* kept,
as Nathan read Gaffiot. The *i* is a vowel (Greek ϊ, its own syllable), so *i*, not *j* (rule 4 makes *j* only of a
consonantal *i*). 1573 prints *Halleluia* at all four places.

**The rule**: word, approved, *halleluja → halleluia*, so the *i → j* rule does not make it *-luja* again (the Psalms).
