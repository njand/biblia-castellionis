# *adstringō*, *īnfestus* and nine settled words: spelling for the whole edition

*2026-11-23.* Raised in 2 Kings' phase 2 (`2kgs1-25-edition.md` points 6–8). Nathan, on Claude's walk-through of the
questions: "Can you make the necessary edits, rather than making me do them? "adstringo" seems to be more common in the
corpus, right? So should we add it to the exceptions? I think I prefer "īnfestus", since the second syllable length is
somewhat uncertain. Apply everything else as recommended."

The printed texts (`text/orig/`) keep Castellio's spelling (decision 58); only the edited text changes.

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-23, the words above; point 1 on the counts below, which bear out his reading)

## The evidence

1. ***adstringō*** keeps *ad-*. Counts from the Latin Library corpus (`python -m pipeline latin attest --near 0
   "<stem>*" --count`): *adstring-* 198, *adstrinx-* 50, *adstrict-* 142 (390 in all; classical, republican, archaic,
   augustan and silver 121); *astring-* 85, *astrinx-* 21, *astrict-* 103 (209; those eras 65). Cicero writes both
   (*adstring-* 9, *astrict-* 19); Ovid, Seneca, Quintilian and Curtius *adstr-*. Gaffiot heads the verb *astringō*;
   the Macronizer and Wiktionary know *adstringō* too. So *adstringō* joins *adquīrō, adsequor, adsignō, adstō,
   adsurgō* (decision 81) and *adsistō, adsector, adstruō* (decision 86). Stem rules *astring-, astrinx-, astrict- →
   adstr…*; 2 Kgs 15:9 *adstrīnxerat* stands; Gen 24:37 *astrīnxit* respelled.
2. ***īnfestus***, ***īnfestō*** with a short *e*. Gaffiot marks *īnfēstus*, *īnfēstō*; Wiktionary *īnfestus*,
   *īnfestō*; the Macronizer both. The vowel before *-st-* is hidden and the authorities disagree; Nathan takes the short
   vowel, as the settled 1 Samuel and 1 Kings (eleven words) already have it. 37 settled words in 14 books respelled
   (§ The settled text), and their forms rows.
3. The rest, as Claude recommended (`2kgs1-25-edition.md`; the walk-through of 2026-11-23):
   - **1 Kgs 19:16 *Nansis* → *Namsis*** (Nimshi): 2 Kings prints *Namsis* three times (9:2, 9:14, 9:20), the Hebrew
     נִמְשִׁי has the *m*, the Greek Ναμεσσί; *Nansis* taken for 1738's slip. The printed text keeps *Nansis*.
   - **Matt 1:9–10 *Ezecīam*, *Ezecīās* → *Ezeciam*, *Ezeciās***: Gaffiot *Ezĕchĭās*, a short *i* (the Hebrew's hireq
     before the doubled yod); 2 Kings' *Ezechiās* the same (its forms rows). Matthew keeps 1573's spelling without *h*.
   - **Gen 31:20, 31:24 *Sȳrō*, *Sȳrum* → *Syrō*, *Syrum***: Gaffiot marks the people *Sўrus* short (only the island
     *Sȳros* long), as every other settled *Syr-* (about 43 words).
   - **Deut 28:55 *arctā* → *artā***: Gaffiot's entry *artus* (*arctus* a variant), the settled Matt 7:14 *arta*; 2 Kgs
     19:3 *artum*. Word rules *arctum → artum*, *arcta → arta*.
   - **Matt 21:17 *diversātus* → *dīversātus***: every other settled form has *dī-* (Acts *dīversātur*, Judges
     *dīversātum*, Ruth *dīversābor*); 2 Kgs 4:10 *dīversētur*.
   - **Acts 21:37 *Scisne* → *Scīsne***: *scīs*, long *ī*; 2 Kgs 2:3 the same.
   - **Judg 4:12 *adscendisse* → *ascendisse***: decision 37, as every other settled form of *ascendō*.
   - 2 Kings words, by rule or forms row (no settled text): *vēniitque*, *vēnīrent* (*vēneō*), *lymphātus*,
     *lautumiīs*; word rules *vaeniitque, vaenirent, limphatus, latumiis*.
