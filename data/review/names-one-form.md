# One form of a name in both Testaments: Hagar and Noah

Proposed 2026-11-11 (Claude), after Nathan revisited section C of `data/review/open-questions-paul.md` ("Doesn't it
make sense to keep them the same?"); decided the same day: **one form per person across the Testaments, the Old
Testament's** (decision 46).

## The evidence

- **Modern translations use one form.** In this repo's copies, the BSB and WEB have *Hagar* in both Gen 16:1 and Gal
  4:24; the NRSV, ESV and NIV do the same (from knowledge; not in this repo). The KJV alone has *Agar* in Galatians, as
  it keeps the New Testament's Greek forms throughout (*Noe*, *Elias*, *Esaias*). The Vulgate has *Agar* in both
  Testaments, after the Septuagint's Ἄγαρ.
- **The sources favour *Hagar*.** The Hebrew is *Hāgār*; Paul's Greek, in the SBLGNT, is Ἁγάρ, with the rough
  breathing. Castellio's own 1573 prints *Hagar* in Genesis (canvases 33, 37) and *Agar* in Galatians (canvas 891), the
  Vulgate's spelling.
- **The settled text**: Genesis declines her *Hagar* (nominative and vocative, 16:8, 21:17), *Hagāris*, *Hagārem*,
  *Hagāre*, *Hagārī* (8 places); Gal 4:24–25 has *Agar* twice, both nominatives. Noah is *Nōa*, *Nōæ*, *Nōam*, *Nōā*
  in Genesis (27 places) and already so in Luke (3:36 and 17:26 *Nōæ*, 17:27 *Nōā*) and in Wikisource's Hebrews 11:7,
  1 Peter 3:20 and 2 Peter 2:5; only Matt 24:37–38 has *Nōē*.
- **Precedent**: decision 37 already spells a name one way across books (*Jōhannēs* in Acts as in the Gospels).

## The changes

**Decision:** accept all (Nathan, 2026-11-11: "Unify Hagar and Noah")

1. **Gal 4:24** *vidēlicet Agar* → *Hagar*, as Genesis's nominative.
   **Apply:** `Gal.4.24` la: `vidēlicet Agar:` → `vidēlicet Hagar:`
2. **Gal 4:25** *Nam Agar* → *Hagar*.
   **Apply:** `Gal.4.25` la: `Nam Agar,` → `Nam Hagar,`
3. **Matt 24:37** *tempore Nōē* → *Nōæ*: the genitive of *Nōa*, as Luke 17:26 *Nōæ temporibus* and Gen 7:11.
   **Apply:** `Matt.24.37` la: `tempore Nōē` → `tempore Nōæ`
4. **Matt 24:38** *quō Nōē in arcam* → *Nōa*: the nominative, as Gen 7:13 *quō Nōa … in arcam*.
   **Apply:** `Matt.24.38` la: `quō Nōē in arcam` → `quō Nōa in arcam`

## For the books still to come

The rule is written into `brief.md` (Proper names). Where Wikisource's later New Testament books spell an Old
Testament person in the Greek or Vulgate way (*Elias*, *Esaias*, *Noe*, *Agar*), the stage-3 reader proposes the Old
Testament's form, in its case, with the reason; no spelling rule does it, since a word rule cannot know the case (and
*agar* is also a verb).
