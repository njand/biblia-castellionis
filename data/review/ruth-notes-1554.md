`gen-exod-notes-1554.md`). Every annotation on Ruth in the 1554 Basel edition was read on the page and sorted as (a) a
deliberate departure from the Hebrew as read today, (b) a word no dictionary has, (c) commentary (no note), or (d) a

## 1. Ruth 3:16 *Illa in oppidum ad socrum venit* (proposed in `ruth-notes.md`, item 1)

The annotation (canvas 825, column 345, *EX CAP. III.*):

> *ILla in oppidum ad ſocrum ue-nit.) Venit in Hebræo maſculinum eſt: ſed mendum eſſe conijcio, tum ex ſenſu, tum ex
> uetere uerſione, ſicuti capite ſequenti יגאל, pro תגאל mẽdoſe legitur.*

("*Venit* is masculine in the Hebrew, but I suspect a mistake, both from the sense and from the old version, just as in
the next chapter יגאל is wrongly read for תגאל.") The Hebrew is printed unpointed; both words read clearly on the zoom.
The lemma is Castellio's 3:16 (the KJV's 3:15 end and 3:16 joined: Hebrew 3:15 ends וַיָּבֹא הָעִיר, "and he went into the
city"; 3:16 begins וַתָּבוֹא אֶל־חֲמוֹתָהּ, "and she came to her mother-in-law"). His "old version" is the Vulgate's *Quæ portans
ingressa est civitatem* (`sources/parallel/vulgate/Ruth.tsv`). The 1554 text has the reading (canvas 152, column 279:
*Illa in op-|pidum ad ſocrum uenit: quærentiq́ quid egiſ-|ſet*), with no mark in the text.

So it is deliberate: Castellio emends the Hebrew's "he" to "she", with the Vulgate. The NRSVue, the BSB and the NET all
keep the Hebrew's "he" (Boaz). Kind C. Proposed with its evidence in `ruth-notes.md` (item 1).

His comparison, 4:4 יִגְאַל ("if *he* will not redeem") for תִּגְאַל ("if *you* …"), is the BSB's and the NRSVue's own
reading there ("Heb mss Gk Syr Vg: MT *if he*"), and his 4:4 *sīn minus redēmptūrus es* has it: no note (`ruth-notes.md` §C).

## The 1554 text's own margins, and 1738's four footnotes

The 1554 Ruth prints **four lettered marginal notes**, all in chapter 1, beside the side-heads (*Noemis in Iudæam
reuertitur.*, *Elimelechus.*, *Boozus.*, *Rutha ſpicas lectitat.*, *Rutha domum redit.*, *Noemis cũ Rutha de uiro ei
conciliando agit.*, *Rutha Boozo petit ut ſe uxorem ducat.*, *Boozus cũ Ruthæ cognato de ea in matrimoniũ ducenda agit.*,
*Rutha ducit Boozum.*) and cross-references (*Deut. 25*, *Gen. 38*, *Mat. 1*). None after 1:20. Three of 1738's four
footnotes are 1554's margins; the fourth (1:8) is a different gloss at the same word, first found in 1573.

| 1:20 | *b) Noemis ab amœnitate et iucunditate: Mara ab acerbitate et mœrore dicitur.* | *d Noemi ab amœnitate & iucunditate: Mara ab acerbitate & mœrore dicitur.* (151, 277; at *Nolite me* ᵈ *Noemin*) | *d Noemis ab amœnitate & iucunditate: Mara ab acerbitate & mœrore dicitur.* |

(The 1573 margins were read on the 1200-px page and, for 1:8 and 1:10, on the zoom; the others not letter by letter.)

- **1:10, 1:13, 1:20 are Castellio's** (1554), changed later only in spelling (*quum*, *numquam*; 1:20 *Noemi* → *Noemis*,
  as 1573 has it).
- **1:8 is not 1554's.** 1554 glosses *mortuos* as "your husbands, while they lived"; 1573 (Perna, *ex postrema
  recognitione*) prints at the same word *me, propter vestros maritos* ("to me, for your husbands' sake", which reads
  as a gloss on *tum ergā mē* rather than on *mortuōs*), and 1738 follows 1573. Whether Castellio himself revised it
  (the 1573 title claims his last revision) or 1573's editor did is not known; 1556 and 1551 were not checked.
- None marks a departure from the Hebrew: all four are glosses on the sense. Decided in `ruth-notes.md` §B.

## The 1554 text against the settled text (beside the notes)

The lemma of the annotation and the text beside each margin match the preview (`text/draft/RUT.preview.usfm`), except
in spelling, with these differences of wording or form, seen on the page:
- **1:18** 1554 *Tum illa uidẽs, eam ſecum obſtinauiſſe profi-|ciſci, contradicere deſijt.* (canvas 151, column 277;
  zoom `n151-ruth1_18.jpg`); **1573** *diſſuadere deſijt* (canvas 186, its verse 19), 1738 *dissuadere desiit*, the
  preview *dissuādēre dēsiit*. A word changed after 1554, by 1573. Not a (d) item: the annotation does not bear on it,
  and the edition follows its base (1738); for `docs/editions.md`.
- **1:20, 1:21** 1554 *Noemin* (accusative, twice); 1573 and 1738 *Noemim*.
- **3:16** 1554 has no mark in the text; 1738 prints an asterisk after *venit:* with no footnote (`ruth1-4-transcription.md`),
  at the very word where Castellio's lemma ends, and 1573 prints this annotation after chapter 3 (below). The asterisk
  may be a trace of the annotation; not proved.

## 1573 prints the annotation in the text

1573 (canvas 187, column 230) sets it under its own head at the end of chapter 3, before *CAPVT IIII.*:

> *ANNOTATIO.* | *16 Illa in oppidum ad ſocrum venit.) Venit, in Hebræo maſculinum eſt: ſed mendum eſſe conijcio, tum
> ex ſenſu, tum ex vetere verſione.*

It is shortened: the comparison with 4:4 is left out. Its lemma carries 1573's verse number (16, as the KJV's).
