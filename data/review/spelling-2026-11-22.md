# *adsistō*, *adsector*, *adstruō*, *commīnctor*, *cæcūtiō*, *Adarezerō*: five spellings for the whole edition

*2026-11-22.* Raised in 1 Kings' phase 2 (`1kgs1-22-edition.md` point 8). Nathan asked what Gaffiot and the corpus
suggest, then answered: "1. Based on the classical corpus evidence, let's add "adsisto" to the exceptions, and fix it
where it was already applied differently. 2. Again, let's go by the classical evidence. Enforce "adsectat-" 3. Can you
just remove the "astrum" and its inflected forms from the corpus count, for a better comparison? My Gaffiot heads it
with "adstruo", which is how I am leaning now. 4. Yes, fix the settled ones. 5. Yes, and fix the settled ones."

This narrows decision 37 (*ad-* assimilated) further, as decision 81 did: ***adsistō*, *adsector* (with *adsectātor*,
*adsectātrīx*) and *adstruō* keep *ad-*** in every form, beside *adquīrō, adsequor, adsignō, adstō, adsurgō*. The
printed texts (`text/orig/`) keep Castellio's spelling (decision 58).

**Decision:** accept (Nathan, 2026-11-22, the words above; point 3 decided on the recount below, which bears out his
leaning: *adstruō*)

## The evidence

Counts from the Latin Library corpus (`python -m pipeline latin attest --near 0 "<stem>*" --count`), every form
beginning with the stem; the macron tool for Gaffiot's headwords and the other dictionaries. Gaffiot heads *ad-*
compounds under the *ad-* form as a rule, so his headword is support, not proof.

1. ***adsistō***: *adsist-* 203 hits, of them tiers 1 to 3 89 (tier 1: 7), Tacitus 25, Quintilian 10, Plautus 6; eras
   classical 50, silver 19, republican 8, archaic 7, augustan 4. *assist-* 179, tiers 1 to 3 28 (tier 1: none), most
   medieval (Albert of Aix, Cassiodorus, William of Tyre) and Erasmus 9; eras classical 3, silver 15, augustan 1.
   Gaffiot *adsistō*; Wiktionary heads *assistō*, *adsistō* its alternative.
2. ***adsector***, ***adsectātor***: *adsect-* (verb and noun) 24 hits, Cicero 10, Tacitus 3; eras republican 10,
   classical 5, silver 2, augustan 1. *assect-* 18 hits, Erasmus 5, Seneca 3, Pliny the Younger 3, Neo-Latin 10; no
   republican or classical hit. Gaffiot *adsector*, *adsectātŏr*; Wiktionary heads *assector*, *assectātor*. So
   1 Kings' *assectātōrēs* and the settled Luke 21:8 *assectārī* take *ad-* (*adsectārī* is the verb whose noun is
   *adsectātor*; "Enforce adsectat-" by the same classical evidence). *assecl- → adsecul-* (approved) is another word.
3. ***adstruō***: *adstru-* 68 hits (tiers 2 to 3: 24; eras classical 9, silver 12, republican 1, augustan 1; Pliny
   the Younger 5, Seneca 3). *astru-* gave 140, but 41 of them are *astrum*, *astrumque* (Manilius 23, Horace 2,
   Vergil); without them the verb has **99**: tier 2 9 (Pliny the Younger 6, Apuleius 2, Erasmus 1), tier 3 1
   (Frontinus), tier 4 89, eras post-classical 89, silver 8, classical 1, neo-Latin 1 (`astru*` less `astrum*`, by
   tier and era). So the classical and silver authors write *adstru-* 23 times to *astru-*'s 9 (Pliny writes both).
   Gaffiot *adstrŭō*; Lewis & Short under *a-strŭo* with classical quotations spelled *adstru-*; Wiktionary and the
   Macronizer *astruō*. 1 Kings 6:5 *adstruēbat* already stands.
4. ***commīnctōrem*** (1 Kgs 14:10, 21:21): no dictionary has *comminctor* (Castellio's agent noun for the Hebrew's
   "one who urinates against the wall"). Its stem: Gaffiot *commingō, mīnxī* and the participle *commīnctus*,
   *mingō, mīnxī, mīnctum*; Wiktionary *mīnctus*, *commīnctus*; the Macronizer *mīnctus*. The *i* is long before
   *-nct-*, as *sānctus*, *jūnctus*.
5. ***cæcūtiēns*** (2 Pet 1:9): Gaffiot *cæcūtĭō*, Lewis & Short *caecūtio*, the Macronizer *caecūtiō*; the settled
   Gen 27:1 *cæcūtīret*, Gen 48:10 and 1 Sam 3:2 *cæcūtientibus*, 1 Kgs 14:4 *cæcūtientis*. 2 Peter's form alone lacks
   the mark.
   ***Adarezerō*** (2 Sam 8:5, 8:10 twice, 10:16): no source knows the name (1738's Hadadezer). It declines in the
   second declension (*Adarezerum* 8:3, *Adarezerī* 8:8), whose dative and ablative *-ō* is long; 1 Kgs 11:23
   *Adarezerō*, and 2 Samuel itself marks the ending on other names (1:1 *Mortuō Saūlō*).

## The rules (`data/rules/orthography.tsv`)

- Set to `rejected`, with Nathan's words: *adsistentes → assistentes*, *adsectatores → assectatores*.
- Added, approved, **stem rules** that respell any assimilated form back, whatever its ending: *assist- → adsist-*,
  *assect- → adsect-*, and for *adstruō* the stems *astrua-, astrue-, astrui-, astruo-, astrux-, astruct- → adstru…*
  (never *astru-* alone, which would take *astrum*). Checked: every word beginning with these stems in Wikisource, the
  read lines, the drafts and the settled text is a form of these verbs (*assistit, assistentēs, assectārī,
  assectātor, assectātōrēs, assectātrīcem*; no *astru-* verb form yet).

## The settled text (ten words in nine verses)

The prefix *ad-* has no long vowel; every other mark is kept.

1. **Exod 18:14** *assistit* → *adsistit*.
    **Apply:** `Exod.18.14` la: `ad vesperum assistit?` → `ad vesperum adsistit?`
2. **Acts 23:4** *assistentēs* → *adsistentēs*.
    **Apply:** `Acts.23.4` la: `Hīc assistentēs dīxērunt` → `Hīc adsistentēs dīxērunt`
3. **Luke 21:8** *assectārī* → *adsectārī*.
    **Apply:** `Luke.21.8` la: `assectārī` → `adsectārī`
4. **1 Kgs 1:2** *assectātōrēs* → *adsectātōrēs*.
    **Apply:** `1Kgs.1.2` la: `ejus assectātōrēs,` → `ejus adsectātōrēs,`
5. **1 Kgs 14:10** *comminctōrem* → *commīnctōrem*.
    **Apply:** `1Kgs.14.10` la: `ad comminctōrem parietis` → `ad commīnctōrem parietis`
6. **1 Kgs 21:21** *comminctōrem* → *commīnctōrem*.
    **Apply:** `1Kgs.21.21` la: `ad comminctōrem parietis` → `ad commīnctōrem parietis`
7. **2 Pet 1:9** *cæcutiēns* → *cæcūtiēns*.
    **Apply:** `2Pet.1.9` la: `cæcus est, cæcutiēns,` → `cæcus est, cæcūtiēns,`
8. **2 Sam 8:5** *Adarezero* → *Adarezerō*.
    **Apply:** `2Sam.8.5` la: `quī Adarezero Sophēnæ` → `quī Adarezerō Sophēnæ`
9. **2 Sam 8:10** *Adarezero* twice → *Adarezerō*.
    **Apply:** `2Sam.8.10` la: `cæsōque Adarezero grātulārētur` → `cæsōque Adarezerō grātulārētur`
    **Apply:** `2Sam.8.10` la: `cum Adarezero intercesserant` → `cum Adarezerō intercesserant`
10. **2 Sam 10:16** *Adarezero* → *Adarezerō*.
    **Apply:** `2Sam.10.16` la: `ab Adarezero Syrī` → `ab Adarezerō Syrī`

The forms rows that decided the old marks are respelled (`1Kgs.forms.tsv` 14:10, 21:21; `2Pet.forms.tsv` 1:9), and
`pipeline gold` rebuilt for the five books.
