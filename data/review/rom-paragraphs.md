# Paragraph breaks for the settled Romans, after 1 Corinthians

Proposed 2026-11-10; accepted by Nathan the same day and applied. Romans was settled with only the BSB's section-heading breaks; 1 Corinthians also took some of
the BSB's ordinary paragraph breaks (`b m`), 24 of the 107 that do not follow poetry (about one in four or five), where
the Latin allowed one. This file proposes the same for Romans. Nothing is applied: Romans is settled, so these wait
for your Decision line.

The candidates are the BSB's `b m` breaks in Romans that are not at verse 1, do not follow BSB poetry, and fall where
Romans has no paragraph yet: 102 verses. Each was read in the Latin of `text/ROM.usfm` (the verse before and the verse
itself) beside the BSB. The criterion is 1 Corinthians': a break where the Latin's previous verse ends a sentence
**and** the verse opens a new step in the argument, a new topic, or a turn to direct address (as 1 Cor 4:6, 7:8–12,
11:23, 14:20, 16:15 there); not before a verse that only explains (*enim*, *nam* giving a reason), answers or
continues a series of questions, or finishes the sentence before it; and the greetings of chapter 16 grouped, not
broken at each name. 23 are proposed (22%; 1 Corinthians took 22%); the other 79 are listed at the end.

## Paragraph breaks from the BSB

**Decision:** accept all (Nathan, 2026-11-10)

   **Apply:** `Rom.1.16` la: `\v 16 Nec enim` → `\p \v 16 Nec enim`

   permīsit*).

3. **Rom 2:25** a new topic, circumcision, after the charge against the Jew of 2:17–24 (*Nam ita dēmum prōdest

5. **Rom 4:18** a new step: Abraham's faith itself, after the promise to his offspring (*Quī Abrāhāmus contrā spem

   **Apply:** `Rom.4.18` la: `\v 18 Quī Abrāhāmus` → `\p \v 18 Quī Abrāhāmus`

8. **Rom 7:4** the application of the marriage example, in direct address (*Ad eundem modum, meī frātrēs*).
   **Apply:** `Rom.7.4` la: `\v 4 Ad eundem` → `\p \v 4 Ad eundem`
9. **Rom 7:14** a new step: the divided self, in the present tense (*Scīmus enim, lēgem esse spīrituālem*).
   **Apply:** `Rom.7.14` la: `\v 14 Scīmus enim,` → `\p \v 14 Scīmus enim,`

14. **Rom 10:5** a new step: the two righteousnesses compared from Moses (*Nam dē eā quidem jūstitiā*).
   **Apply:** `Rom.10.5` la: `\v 5 Nam dē` → `\p \v 5 Nam dē`
